What if no driver were distracted

If nobody drove distracted, how many fewer people would die on Spain's roads, and where? The police record of fatal crashes and the risk measured with cameras in cars give the answer.

If no driver on Spain's roads were distracted, about 266 fewer people a year would die, 15% of the 1,779; 225 of them on interurban roads, where the police record distraction in 35% of fatal crashes, against 42 in towns, where they record it in 17%. Distraction is present in more fatal crashes than any other recorded factor, but a distracted driver is only about twice as likely to crash as an attentive one, so only half of those crashes are caused by it. If every distracted driver had been on a handheld phone, which multiplies the risk by 3.6, the figure would be 385; and if the police record misses as much distraction as cameras in cars suggest, it could be as high as 640.

What the model is

  1. Deaths. People killed within 30 days on each kind of road, as drivers, passengers and pedestrians, the mean of 2022–2024 in DGT's yearbook.
  2. How often distraction is there. The share of fatal crashes in which the police recorded distraction as a concurrent factor: 30% on all roads in 2022, 2024 and 2023 (31%, 30% and 30%), 35% on interurban roads and 17% on urban streets, from DGT's table of concurrent factors.
  3. How much it raises the risk. In the largest naturalistic driving study, with cameras in 3,500 cars, a driver doing anything that took attention off the road was 2 times as likely to crash; on a handheld phone, 3.6 times.
  4. The deaths it causes. A factor that multiplies the risk by 2 causes 1 − 1/2 = 50% of the crashes it is present in. Deaths × share with distraction × 50% gives the deaths that would not happen without it.

Two ways to count distraction

The police record distraction in 30% of fatal crashes. In the naturalistic study, where cameras watched the driver, it was present in 68% of crashes, and the authors estimate that 36% of all crashes would not happen without it. The two differ because a police officer reconstructs a crash afterwards and can record distraction only when a witness, a phone record or the driver establishes it, while a camera sees every glance away. The naturalistic figure is American and counts mostly minor crashes, so the page does not use it as its estimate. It uses it as a ceiling: if the police record misses as much distraction as the cameras suggest, distraction would account for up to 640 deaths a year, 462 of them on interurban roads. The truth lies between the record and the ceiling, and nearer the record for the most serious crashes, which are investigated most thoroughly.

What the model concludes

  1. Distraction causes about one road death in 7. 266 a year (266 to 385), 15% of 1,779, on the police record; up to 640 if the record misses as much as the cameras suggest.
  2. Most of the gain is on interurban roads. 225 of the 266: the police record distraction in 35% of fatal crashes there, twice the share in towns (17%), and interurban roads carry 72% of the deaths.
  3. It is mostly drivers who die. 181 of the 266 deaths avoided are drivers, often the distracted driver; pedestrians are 40.
  4. Crashes fall more than deaths. About 6,582 of the 13,164 injury crashes a year with distraction recorded would not happen: distraction is recorded in a larger share of injury crashes than of any other factor, so its weight in crashes is larger than in deaths.

Conclusion

Removing distraction would save about 266 lives a year in Spain (266 to 385), 225 of them on interurban roads, and avoid about 6,582 injury crashes. Distraction is the factor the police record most often in fatal crashes but not the deadliest, because it doubles the risk where alcohol multiplies it many times. The police record is a floor: on what cameras in cars see, the figure could be up to 640.

Limits. The police record is a judgement made after the crash and covers Spain without Cataluña and País Vasco; the urban share is the difference between all roads and interurban roads, whose percentage DGT rounds. The risk comes from one American naturalistic study of crashes of every severity; fatal crashes may differ. Removing a share of distraction is taken to remove the same share of its crashes. A crash with several factors counts fully here and on the other factor pages, so the factors cannot be added; the enforcement page combines them.
