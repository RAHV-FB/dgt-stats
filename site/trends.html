Spain

Road deaths and injuries, 2019–2024

Deaths, hospital admissions and injury crashes in Spain in 2024 compared with 2019, as counts and as rates per resident, licence holder, registered vehicle and tonne of road fuel sold.

Spain recorded 1,785 road deaths in 2024, 1.7% more than in 2019. As a rate, the change depends on what the deaths are divided by: per resident they fell 1.9%, and per tonne of road fuel sold they rose 3.5%. Under every denominator, including drivers per licence holder and vehicle occupants per registered vehicle, the change lies within the ordinary year-to-year variation of the annual count. The number of people admitted to hospital after a crash rose beyond it: 11.0% as a count and 13.0% per tonne of road fuel.

Ordinary year-to-year variation

If road casualties were independent events, each occurring by chance, a year's count would scatter around its trend with a variance equal to its mean, the pattern known as Poisson variation. Spain's annual counts scatter more than that. Over 2013–2019, the last segment of the long-run trend before the pandemic, the variance around the trend was 1.5 times the Poisson variance for deaths, 7.7 times for hospital admissions and 68 times for injury crashes. Each interval in the table is widened by the square root of its own count's factor, so that it spans the variation of an ordinary year: a change outside it is larger than the count's ordinary movement from one year to the next.

The widening matters most for injury crashes, whose intervals are about 8 times as wide as Poisson intervals (the square root of 68). Injury crashes fell 2.0% as a count, 5.5% per resident and 0.3% per tonne of road fuel. A pure Poisson interval would treat the falls as a count and per resident as real changes; against ordinary variation none of them stands out. DGT's yearbook does not split injury crashes by vehicle type, so they have no rate per licence holder or registered vehicle.

Per resident, and for drivers per licence holder and occupants per registered vehicle, the rise in hospital admissions stays within ordinary variation. Admissions rose while the number of injury crashes fell, so more people were admitted to hospital per crash. Two explanations fit these tables: crashes became more serious, or admissions were traced back to crashes more completely. A change in tracing would move the series without any change on the road, and the tables do not distinguish the two.

Denominators and what they measure

Each denominator measures a different opportunity for harm. Residents measure a population: deaths per resident describe the burden of road deaths on everyone living in Spain, whether or not they travel. Licence holders and registered vehicles measure who or what could be on the road, without saying how much each is used. Road fuel sold comes closest to measuring traffic itself. A denominator can be set only against the casualties it could contain: pedestrians and cyclists hold no licence for their journey and travel in no registered vehicle, so licence holders divide only the deaths of drivers, and the fleet only those of occupants, of motorcycles, cars, vans, trucks and buses. Mopeds, bicycles, personal mobility vehicles and the yearbook's category of other vehicles are left out of both, because they need no licence, may be missing from the fleet or were reclassified during the period.

Between 2019 and 2024 the denominators themselves moved apart: the population grew 3.7%, licence holders 3.0% and the registered fleet 5.2%, while road fuel sold fell 1.7%. The same deaths therefore fall per resident and rise per tonne of fuel. Both rates are correct, and they describe different things: the burden on a population, and deaths in proportion to the traffic that fuel stands for. A change that appears under one denominator and reverses under another says more about the denominators than about the roads.

Road fuel as a measure of traffic

Risk on the road is best expressed per kilometre travelled, but no Spanish source counts vehicle-kilometres on all roads every year, and road fuel sold stands in for them. The kilometres a tonne represents can change with fuel economy, electric driving, the mix of freight and private travel, and fuel bought in Spain but burnt elsewhere, and no available series measures that drift on all roads. The change per tonne of fuel therefore cannot be converted into a change per kilometre. A hypothetical case shows how much depends on it: had kilometres per tonne grown 1% a year since 2019, deaths per tonne of fuel would show a fall of 1.5% instead of a rise of 3.5%; at 2% a year, a fall of 6.2%. These growth rates are illustrative assumptions. Long-run trends sets the latest years against the pre-pandemic trend, per tonne of fuel and per measured interurban kilometre.

DGT has published estimates of vehicle-kilometres for two years, 2022 and 2024. They are built by different methods and cannot be joined into a series: they give 1.6% fewer kilometres in 2024 than in 2022 (3.3% fewer for cars), while road fuel sold rose 1.5%. Each is used where a single year is enough: 2022 for vehicle types and 2024 for drivers by age.

Limitations. The denominators are national totals that weight every resident, licence, vehicle and tonne of fuel alike. The intervals allow for the year-to-year variation of the casualty counts but treat every denominator as exact.
