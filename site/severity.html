Crash circumstances and fatal outcomes

A logistic regression relates the circumstances recorded for each injury crash in DGT's national records, 2016–2024, to whether anyone died.

Among injury crashes, those in wet conditions and those at a junction were less often fatal than comparable crashes on a dry road or away from a junction. The comparison uses odds ratios: the odds of a death with a circumstance present divided by the odds without it, the other recorded circumstances held equal, so that 1 means no difference and a value below 1 a lower chance of death. Wet conditions, counted once rather than split between rain and a wet road surface, go with 0.56× the odds of a death, and a junction with 0.75×. The highest odds ratios belong to two kinds of crash: compared with a side collision, a head-on collision has 5.66× the odds of a death and a pedestrian struck 6.44×. The analysis concerns how a crash ends, given an injury crash. It says nothing about how often crashes happen. These are associations in DGT's national crash records, which are not used to train a predictive model (Data sources and scope).

The records and the regression

DGT's national records hold 875,013 injury crashes for 2016–2024, of which 1.6% had at least one death within 30 days. The file has one row per crash and no fields for drivers, vehicles or people. Two logistic regressions are fitted, one for at least one death within 30 days and one for a death or a hospitalisation. Their predictors are the circumstances the police record: zone, road type, crash type, junction, lighting, weather, road surface, alignment, time of day, weekend, number of vehicles and year. A missing value is kept as a level of its own, so no crash is dropped, and levels with fewer than 500 crashes are merged into their reference category. The intervals are 95% confidence intervals, with standard errors clustered by province.

Wet conditions and junctions

Weather and road surface record much the same thing, so a model that contains both divides one association between them. With road surface left out, the odds ratio for rain moves from 0.86 (0.72–1.03) to 0.56 (0.49–0.65); with weather left out, the odds ratio for a wet surface moves from 0.62 (0.52–0.74) to 0.56 (0.49–0.64). Rain and a wet surface are therefore one wet-conditions association of about 0.56, the figure given above. The full model's 0.62 for a wet surface, with rain alongside it, is the value in the tables below and in the refit without Catalonia.

Wet weather is also unevenly spread across roads: 49% of crashes in the rain are on interurban roads, against 36% of all crashes. Fitting interurban roads and urban streets separately holds that context fixed: the wet-surface odds ratio is 0.63 (0.54–0.74) on interurban roads and 0.57 (0.39–0.82) on urban streets. Its interval lies below 1 in every model variant, and so does that of the junction association, 0.75 (0.69–0.81) in the full model.

Hail and snow behave differently. Their odds ratio is 0.63 (0.46–0.85) in the full model but 0.99 (0.62–1.57) on conventional roads alone, where the interval includes 1. Hail and snow are recorded in only 1,577 crashes, 80% of them on interurban roads, so a handful of provinces could drive the result. Removing the three provinces that record most of them leaves the odds ratio at 0.66 (0.46–0.93), inside the full model's interval. Only the restriction to conventional roads removes the association; removing those provinces barely moves it.

Missing values and regional recording

Some levels stand for a missing value, a field's “unknown” or “not specified” code. They stay in the regression but are left out of the figures and of the results in the text. One of them is among the strongest terms in the whole regression, and it sits almost entirely in one region. Road alignment “unknown”, with an odds ratio of 0.12, is recorded for 34.7% of crashes in Catalonia against 0.5% elsewhere: 95.7% of its 78,990 crashes are Catalan, although Catalonia has 24.9% of all crashes. The odds ratio marks crashes recorded mostly by Catalonia's police forces: it describes recording practice. The other missing-value levels are almost all outside Catalonia (at most 0.07% Catalan).

A recording practice that goes with the outcome could distort the other coefficients through such a level, so the fatal-outcome regression was refitted without Catalonia's 217,966 crashes. All 32 other odds ratios stay inside the full model's interval. The largest moves are in zone and road type, which have to be read together: for the zone “urban crossing”, the odds ratio goes from 4.18 to 2.57. The wet-surface odds ratio moves from 0.62 to 0.72 and the junction odds ratio from 0.75 to 0.70, both still below 1, while alignment “unknown” itself moves from 0.12 to 0.44 once most of its crashes are gone.

Stability over time

Fitted on 2016–2022 without a year term and applied to the crashes of 2023–2024, the fatal-outcome regression keeps its ordering of crashes: ROC-AUC 0.80 for a death and 0.69 for a death or a hospitalisation (Methodology). The Catalonia severity model's ROC-AUC of 0.79 measures a different task: there a death is picked out among crashes with a death or serious injury, a population already selected for severity; here, among all injury crashes, of which 1.6% were fatal. As probabilities, the fitted values improve little on giving every crash the training years' share of each outcome: their Brier score (mean squared error) is 4.2% lower for a death and 5.3% lower for a death or a hospitalisation.

The regression was also refitted one year at a time. Of the 90 yearly estimates for its 10 crash-type, zone and road-type terms, 32 fall outside the full model's interval, more in 2023 and 2024 than in any other year (8 and 7): the years in which the coding of junctions and of urban road types changed (coding breaks). Road type accounts for 20 of the 32.

Severity and frequency

A circumstance can raise the number of crashes and lower the share that are fatal at the same time, and these records observe only the second: they contain no measure of how much driving takes place on wet roads or through junctions. Why the fatal share is lower is also untested, since the file has no data on speed, vehicles or drivers. Two associations hold throughout: wet conditions and junctions go with lower odds that an injury crash is fatal in every model variant and in the refit without Catalonia's crashes. The association with hail and snow disappears on conventional roads and remains unresolved.
