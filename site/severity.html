Crash severity

Crash circumstances and fatal outcomes

In Spain's 2016–2024 injury crashes, other recorded circumstances held equal, a head-on collision had 5.48 times the odds of a death within 30 days (against a side or front-side collision) and a crash on a conventional interurban road 6.63 times the odds (against an urban street). With an inverted Catalan junction flag read the other way round, crashes at junctions (0.69, 0.65–0.73) and on wet roads (0.62, 0.52–0.74) had lower odds; this says nothing about how often crashes happen there. Catalonia (deaths within 24 hours) and Barcelona cover other crashes.

Crash type and location go most strongly with a death

Of the 875,013 injury crashes in DGT's national records for 2016–2024, 1.6% had at least one death within 30 days. An odds ratio compares the odds of a death in crashes with a circumstance against those at its reference level, with the other recorded circumstances held equal; 1 means no difference. Outside zone and road type, a pedestrian struck (6.27, 5.22–7.53) and a head-on collision (5.48, 4.95–6.06) stand far above every other circumstance; the next strongest is the crash type “run-off or overturn”, at 2.05 (1.66–2.52).

Zone and road type describe one location between them, so the table adds the two together. Against a crash on an urban street, a crash on any of the four interurban road types had 5.66 to 7.23 times the odds of a death, and a crash on a conventional road where it runs through a town (the zone DGT calls an urban crossing) 8.75 times.

Lower odds of a death on wet roads and at junctions

In the full model a wet surface had 0.62 (0.52–0.74) times the odds of a death and rain 0.86 (0.73–1.03). Weather and road surface record much the same thing, so a model that contains both divides one association between them: with weather left out a wet surface goes with 0.56, and with surface left out rain with 0.56. The wet-surface interval lies below 1 in every model variant, including interurban roads (0.63, 0.54–0.74) and urban streets (0.57, 0.39–0.82) fitted separately.

A crash at a junction had 0.69 (0.65–0.73) times the odds of a death, below 1 in every variant. In 2023 and 2024 DGT's records for the four Catalan provinces code the junction flag the wrong way round (coding breaks), so the analysis reads it the other way round for these 47,705 crashes. Fitted on each period apart, the odds ratio is 0.69 (0.65–0.72) in 2016–2022 and 0.72 (0.65–0.79) in 2023–2024; with the flag as published it is 0.75 (0.69–0.81).

The records show which crashes were deadlier, not how often crashes happen or why

A circumstance can raise the number of crashes and lower the share that are fatal at the same time, and these records observe only the fatal share: they contain no measure of how much driving takes place on wet roads or through junctions. Why crashes in wet conditions or at junctions are less often fatal is also untested, since the file has no data on speed or on the drivers.

The two regressions, one for a death within 30 days and one for a death or a hospitalisation, describe associations only. The file's fields are recorded too unevenly between provinces to train a predictive model (data sources and scope), and the regressions are not used to predict, as a matter of purpose. How they are specified and checked is set out in the technical notes.
