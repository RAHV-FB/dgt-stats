Crash severity

Crash circumstances and fatal outcomes

DGT's national records hold 875,013 injury crashes in Spain for 2016–2024; 1.6% had at least one death within 30 days. With the other recorded circumstances held equal, the odds of a death went most strongly with the type of crash and where it happened. Against a side or front-side collision, a head-on collision had 5.66 times the odds of a death (95% interval 5.16–6.22) and a pedestrian struck 6.44 times (5.42–7.65); against a crash on an urban street, a crash on a conventional interurban road had 6.69 times the odds (4.96–9.02). Wet conditions went with lower odds (0.56 times the odds, 0.49–0.64), and so did junctions in 2016–2022 (0.69, 0.65–0.72) but not in 2023–2024 (0.98, 0.76–1.28), after the records for the Catalan provinces began to code the junction flag the wrong way round. These associations among crashes that happened say nothing about how often crashes happen or why some are deadlier. The pages on Catalonia (crashes with a death or serious injury, deaths within 24 hours) and Barcelona (every crash the city police attended in one year) cover other crashes with other definitions, so their figures differ.

Crash type and location go most strongly with a death

An odds ratio compares the odds of a death in crashes with a circumstance against those in crashes at its reference level, with the other recorded circumstances held equal; 1 means no difference. The chart gives the odds ratio of every circumstance the police record. Outside zone and road type, a head-on collision and a pedestrian struck stand far above the rest: the next strongest is the crash type “run-off or overturn”, at 2.11 (1.74–2.55).

Zone and road type describe one location between them, so the chart shows each against the other's reference, and the table below adds the two together. Against a crash on an urban street, a crash on any of the four interurban road types had 5.72 to 7.34 times the odds of a death, and a crash on a conventional road where it runs through a town (the zone DGT calls an urban crossing) 8.74 times. The road type “other” mixes two kinds of crash: in 2024 DGT's records for the four Catalan provinces code urban streets as another kind of road (coding breaks), so its odds ratio, 1.79, rises to 2.40 without Catalonia's crashes.

Lower odds of a death on wet roads, and at junctions until the coding changed

Weather and road surface record much the same thing, so a model that contains both divides one association between them. With road surface left out, the odds ratio for rain moves from 0.86 (0.72–1.03) to 0.56 (0.49–0.65); with weather left out, the odds ratio for a wet surface moves from 0.62 (0.52–0.74) to 0.56 (0.49–0.64). Rain and a wet surface are therefore one association of about 0.56 for wet conditions. The full model's 0.62 for a wet surface, with rain alongside it, is the value in the tables below.

Wet weather is also unevenly spread across roads: 49% of crashes in the rain are on interurban roads, against 36% of all crashes. Fitting interurban roads and urban streets separately holds that context fixed: the wet-surface odds ratio is 0.63 (0.54–0.74) on interurban roads and 0.57 (0.39–0.82) on urban streets. Its interval lies below 1 in every model variant.

The junction association is below 1 in every variant too, at 0.75 (0.69–0.81) in the full model, but that figure pools records whose junction flag is the wrong way round with the rest. From 2023 the records for the Catalan provinces code crashes between junctions as at a junction and the reverse (coding breaks). The share of their crashes recorded at a junction went from 40% in 2016–2022 to 63% in 2023–2024, against 39% and 38% elsewhere. Fitted on each period apart, crashes at a junction had 0.69 (0.65–0.72) times the odds of a death in 2016–2022 and 0.98 (0.76–1.28) in 2023–2024. Outside Catalonia the later figure is 0.74 (0.66–0.83), so the association disappears only where the flag is inverted. The results for junctions are read from the earlier years.

Hail and snow behave differently. Their odds ratio is 0.63 (0.46–0.85) in the full model but 0.99 (0.62–1.57) on conventional roads alone, where the interval includes 1. Hail and snow are recorded in only 1,577 crashes, 80% of them on interurban roads, so a handful of provinces could drive the result. Removing the three provinces that record most of them leaves the odds ratio at 0.66 (0.46–0.93), inside the full model's interval.

What the records cannot show

A circumstance can raise the number of crashes and lower the share that are fatal at the same time, and these records observe only the fatal share: they contain no measure of how much driving takes place on wet roads or through junctions. Why crashes in wet conditions or at junctions are less often fatal is also untested, since the file has no data on speed or on the drivers.

The records and the regressions

DGT's file has one row per injury crash, with counts of the people killed and injured, but no record of individual drivers, vehicles or people. Its fields are recorded too unevenly between provinces to train a predictive model (data sources and scope), but they can describe associations. Two logistic regressions are fitted, one for at least one death within 30 days and one for a death or a hospitalisation. Their circumstances are those the police record: zone, road type, crash type, junction, lighting, weather, road surface, alignment, time of day, weekend (from 20:00 on Friday to the end of Sunday), number of vehicles and year. A missing value is kept as a level of its own, so no crash is dropped, and levels with fewer than 500 crashes are merged into their reference category. The intervals are 95% confidence intervals, with standard errors clustered by province.

How stable the associations are over time

Fitted on 2016–2022 without a year term and applied to the crashes of 2023–2024, the two regressions keep their ordering of crashes. Given one crash with the outcome and one without, the share of such pairs in which a regression gives the crash with the outcome the higher probability is its ROC-AUC, from 0.5 for chance to 1 for a perfect ranking: 0.80 for the regression for a death and 0.69 for a death or a hospitalisation. Part of that ordering comes from how the form was filled in rather than from the crash: refitted with every level that records a missing value folded into its reference, the regression for a death scores 0.78, and those levels on their own 0.54. Across the wider set of fields the DGT microdata audit examines, which fields were left unrecorded scores 0.68 on its own, one reason the file is not used to train a predictive model. The Catalan severity model scores 0.74 on years it had not seen, but among crashes already selected for a death or serious injury; here the deaths are picked out among all injury crashes, of which 1.6% were fatal. As probabilities, the fitted values improve little on giving every crash the training years' share of each outcome: their Brier score (mean squared error) is 4.2% lower for a death and 5.3% lower for a death or a hospitalisation.

Refitted one year at a time, 39 of the 108 yearly estimates for the regression's 10 largest terms (each a crash-type, zone or road-type term) and its junction and wet-surface terms fall outside the full model's interval. Each year's estimate has its own sampling error, so some departures are expected: the full model's value lies outside the year's own interval for 18. Road type accounts for 20 of the 39; its odds ratios swing from year to year against the zone odds ratios, the two splitting one location contrast between them. Tested against their yearly errors (Cochran's Q), only the junction term varies between years by more than chance: its odds ratio lies between 0.63 and 0.79 in every year to 2022 and is 1.00 in 2023 and 2024, the years of the new junction coding (coding breaks).

Missing values and regional recording

Some levels stand for a missing value, a field's “unknown” or “not specified” code. They stay in the regression but are left out of the charts and of the results in the text. One of them is among the strongest terms in the whole regression, and it sits almost entirely in one region. Road alignment “unknown”, with an odds ratio of 0.12, is recorded for 34.7% of crashes in Catalonia against 0.5% elsewhere: 95.7% of its 78,990 crashes are Catalan, although Catalonia has 24.9% of all crashes. The odds ratio marks crashes recorded mostly by Catalonia's police forces: it describes recording practice. The other missing-value levels are almost all outside Catalonia (at most 0.07% Catalan).

A recording practice that goes with the outcome could distort the other coefficients through such a level, so the regression for a death was refitted without Catalonia's 217,966 crashes. All 32 other odds ratios stay inside the full model's interval. The largest moves are in zone and road type, which have to be read together: for the zone “urban crossing”, the odds ratio goes from 4.18 to 2.57. The wet-surface odds ratio moves from 0.62 to 0.72 and the junction odds ratio from 0.75 to 0.70, both still below 1, while alignment “unknown” itself moves from 0.12 to 0.44 once most of its crashes are gone.
