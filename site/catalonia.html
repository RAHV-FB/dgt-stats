Crash severity

Serious and fatal crashes in Catalonia, 2010–2023

In 2010–2023 the Servei Català de Trànsit recorded 24,478 crashes in Catalonia that killed or seriously injured someone. In 3,093 (12.6%) someone died within 24 hours. The fatal share was 19.2% on interurban roads, almost three times the 6.9% on urban streets, and 27.8% when a heavy vehicle was involved. These shares measure how often serious crashes were fatal, not how often crashes happen. Spain's injury crashes (deaths within 30 days) and Barcelona's records cover other crashes, so their shares differ.

Crashes were most often fatal on interurban roads, with a heavy vehicle and on unlit roads at night

Each share looks at one circumstance at a time, and the groups overlap: a crash on an unlit interurban road involving a heavy vehicle belongs to all three, so no share isolates the part one circumstance plays. The crash severity calculator, built on this file, estimates the fatal share for a combination of circumstances, each adjusted for the others.

Most fatal crashes were on conventional roads: 1,981 of the 3,093, with 18.8% of the 10,540 serious crashes on those roads fatal. Roads through towns lay between interurban roads and urban streets, at 11.6%. Motorways had the highest share of any road type (20.5%), but from only 224 crashes, and its 95% interval (15.8–26.3%) overlaps that of conventional roads.

A share can rank groups differently from their number of crashes. Motorcycles were involved in 9,457 crashes, more than any vehicle but light vehicles, yet only 9.9% were fatal, below the average of 12.6%. Heavy vehicles were involved in far fewer (2,717), but 27.8% of those crashes were fatal, more than twice the average.

At night, crashes on roads without street lighting were fatal in 28.1% of cases and those under adequate street lighting in 9.8%, the highest and lowest shares of any lighting condition. Head-on collisions were the most often fatal type of crash (25.0%). Away from a junction 14.8% of crashes were fatal, inside one 7.7%. Every share, with its count and interval, is in the technical notes.

The fatal share rises with the posted speed limit

The speed-limit field records the limit signposted on the road, not how fast anyone was driving; no file in the study records vehicle speeds. Where a limit was posted, the fatal share rises with it, from 7.4% at 10–30 km/h to 23.9% at 100–120 km/h. Higher limits are posted on faster, mostly interurban roads, so the rise describes the roads as much as the limits. In 17,969 crashes (73.4%) no limit was posted and the generic limit for the type of road applied.

Fewest crashes in 2020, most per resident in Lleida

The number of crashes with a death or serious injury fell from 1,984 in 2010 to 1,208 in 2020, the lowest of the period, and was 1,732 in 2023.

These counts agree with DGT's national records, compared by province and year: in all 32 province-years of 2016–2023 the file's fatal crashes equal DGT's crashes with a death within 24 hours. The file does not state its definition of a fatal crash; this agreement shows it.

Relative to population, such crashes were most frequent in the province of Lleida in every year: 42.5 a year per 100,000 residents over 2010–2023, followed by Girona (32.3), Tarragona (29.9) and Barcelona (19.3). Crashes are counted where they happened and residents where they live, so visitors and through traffic count in a province's crashes but not in its population. The file has no measure of how far people travel.

Limitations. The file describes crashes only: it has no records of the people involved and no alcohol or drug test results.
