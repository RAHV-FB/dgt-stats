Catalonia: crashes with a death or serious injury

Catalonia publishes one record for every crash with a death or a serious injury: 24,478 crashes from 2010 to 2023. Because the file holds only serious and fatal crashes, it answers two questions: how many such crashes were recorded (frequency), and how often a serious crash was fatal (severity among them). Overall 12.6% were fatal. That share ranges from 6.9% on urban streets to 20.5% on motorways, is 27.8% when a heavy vehicle was involved and 9.9% when a motorcycle was. It cannot say how likely a crash is to happen, or to become serious: the file has no minor crashes and no exposure.

Severity among recorded serious crashes, not risk. A higher fatal share on a kind of road means that, once a crash with a death or serious injury happened there, it was more often fatal. It says nothing about how often crashes happen on that road, and the differences are associations, not effects.

The speed-limit field is not a speed

C_VELOCITAT_VIA is the limit that applied to the road, not how fast anyone drove. It is a usable number only where the limit was signposted: under the generic limit for the road (Genérica via, 17,969 crashes) the field holds 100, 999 or NA whatever the road, 100 even on urban streets, so it is a code. Where a limit was posted, the fatal share rises with it, from 7.4% at 10-30 km/h to 23.9% at 100-120 km/h; posted limits differ by kind of road, so this mixes road and limit.

How the file lines up with the national data

No Catalan crash is matched to any other record: the file has no identifier that another source shares. The four demarcations are the four provinces, so counts can be compared by province and year. Over 2016–2023 the number of fatal crashes equals the DGT microdata's crashes with a death within 24 hours in every one of the 32 province-years, and the number of crashes equals DGT's crashes with a death or serious injury within 24 hours to within one crash in 32 of them. Against DGT's 30-day deaths the fatal count is 67%–95% as large. The file's severity therefore behaves as the 24-hour definition, and it is compared with national figures only on that basis.

What the file records unevenly

Some fields are left blank or "not specified" at very different rates for serious and for fatal crashes. For D_CARRIL_ESPECIAL a blank covers 7.7% of serious crashes and 1.0% of fatal ones. The file does not say why; whatever the reason, the placeholder itself carries information about the outcome, so the severity model leaves these fields out of its main version: D_CARACT_ENTORN, D_CARRIL_ESPECIAL, D_INFLUIT_BOIRA, D_INFLUIT_CARACT_ENTORN, D_INFLUIT_INTEN_VENT, D_INFLUIT_VISIBILITAT, D_SENTITS_VIA, D_TRACAT_ALTIMETRIC. Recording also differs by place and year (see the data-quality report).

Conclusion

Among Catalan crashes with a death or serious injury, 12.6% were fatal, and the share varies more than twofold with the kind of road and with the vehicles involved. The file's fatal counts are the DGT's 24-hour counts, province by province. It measures severity among serious crashes; it does not measure risk.

Limits. Only crashes with a death or serious injury; no person records, no vehicle speeds, no alcohol or drug tests; no exposure, so no risk; the 100 km/h code; recording differences between places and years. Checks: 0 duplicated rows, every severity label consistent with the casualty counts (see the data-quality report).
