Crash severity

Serious and fatal crashes in Catalonia, 2010–2023

The Servei Català de Trànsit's file holds the 24,478 crashes in Catalonia in 2010–2023 in which someone was killed or seriously injured; crashes with only slight injuries are not in it. A crash counts as fatal when someone died within 24 hours: the file's fatal crashes equal DGT's crashes with a death within 24 hours in every province and year both cover (2016–2023). Of these crashes, 3,093 (12.6%) were fatal. The fatal share was 19.2% on interurban roads, almost three times the 6.9% on urban streets, and it reached 27.8% when a heavy vehicle was involved and 28.1% at night on roads without street lighting. Every crash here was already serious, so these shares describe severity among serious crashes, not the chance that a journey or an ordinary crash ends in a death. The pages on crash circumstances in Spain (every injury crash, deaths within 30 days) and Barcelona (every crash the city police attended in one year) cover other crashes with other definitions, so their figures differ.

Fatal share by road, vehicle and circumstance

Each share below looks at one circumstance at a time, and the groups overlap: a crash on an unlit interurban road involving a heavy vehicle belongs to several at once, so a share does not separate the part played by any one circumstance. The severity model and calculator, fitted on this file, estimates the fatal share for a combination of circumstances, each adjusted for the others.

Most fatal crashes were on conventional roads: 1,981 of the file's 3,093, with 18.8% of the 10,540 serious crashes on those roads fatal. Motorways have the highest fatal share of any road type (20.5%), but it rests on only 224 crashes and its 95% interval (15.8–26.3%) overlaps that of conventional roads. Crashes on roads through towns were fatal in 11.6% of cases, between interurban roads and urban streets. By type of crash, head-on collisions were the most often fatal (25.0%).

A fatal share can rank groups quite differently from their number of crashes. Motorcycles were involved in 9,457 of the file's crashes, more than any other type of vehicle except light vehicles, yet only 9.9% of those crashes were fatal, below the average of 12.6%. Heavy vehicles were involved in far fewer (2,717), but 27.8% of them were fatal, more than twice the average.

At night, crashes on roads without street lighting were fatal in 28.1% of cases and those under adequate street lighting in 9.8%, the highest and lowest shares of any lighting condition. Away from a junction, 14.8% of crashes were fatal; inside a junction, 7.7%.

The fatal share rises with the posted speed limit

The speed-limit field records the limit signposted on the road, not how fast anyone was driving; no file in the study records vehicle speeds. In 17,969 crashes (73.4% of the file) no specific limit was posted and the generic limit for the type of road applied. Where a limit was posted, the fatal share rises with it, from 7.4% at 10–30 km/h to 23.9% at 100–120 km/h. Higher limits are posted on faster, mostly interurban roads, so the rise describes the roads as much as the limits.

Fewest crashes in 2020, most per resident in Lleida

The number of crashes with a death or serious injury fell from 1,984 in 2010 to 1,208 in 2020, the lowest of the period, and was 1,732 in 2023. Relative to population, such crashes were most frequent in the province of Lleida in every year. Over 2010–2023 it recorded 42.5 a year per 100,000 residents, followed by the provinces of Girona (32.3), Tarragona (29.9) and Barcelona (19.3). The crashes are counted where they happened and the residents where they live, so visitors and through traffic count in a province's crashes but not in its population. These are rates per resident; the file has no measure of how far people travel.

How the file's counts compare with DGT's national records

The Catalan file shares no record identifier with DGT's national crash records, so the two are compared by year and province (the file's traffic demarcations). Over 2016–2023, the file's fatal crashes equal DGT's crashes with a death within 24 hours in all 32 province-years, and its total count matches DGT's 24-hour count of crashes with a death or serious injury to within one crash in every province-year. Its fatal count is 67–95% of DGT's count of crashes with a death within 30 days. The file does not state its definition; the counts show that it is the 24-hour one.

Fields left blank more often in non-fatal crashes

The recording check, run on the crashes of 2010–2022, finds eight fields that are left blank or marked “not specified” much more often in non-fatal crashes than in fatal ones. The field for special lanes, for example, is blank in 7.7% of non-fatal crashes and 1.0% of fatal ones. The file gives no reason, but the blank itself carries information about the outcome, and none of these fields is used on this page. Otherwise the file is consistent: it has no duplicated rows, and every severity label agrees with the deaths and injuries recorded on the same row. The repository's data-quality report documents these checks.
