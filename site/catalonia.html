Serious and fatal crashes in Catalonia, 2010–2023

Crashes in which someone was killed or seriously injured in Catalonia, from the Servei Català de Trànsit's file: how often they were fatal, and in which circumstances.

The Catalan file holds 24,478 crashes with a death or serious injury recorded between 2010 and 2023. It contains only such crashes, so the percentages on this page describe severity among crashes that were already serious, not the chance that a journey or an ordinary crash ends in a death. Of these crashes, 3,093 (12.6%) were fatal, with a death within 24 hours. The fatal share was 19.2% on interurban roads, almost three times the 6.9% on urban streets, and it reached 27.8% when a heavy vehicle was involved and 28.1% at night on roads without street lighting.

Fatal share by road, vehicle and circumstance

Most fatal crashes were on conventional roads: 1,981 of the file's 3,093, with 18.8% of the 10,540 serious crashes on those roads fatal. Motorways have the highest fatal share of any road type (20.5%), but it rests on only 224 crashes and its 95% interval (15.8–26.3%) overlaps that of conventional roads. Crashes on roads through towns were fatal in 11.6% of cases, between interurban roads and urban streets. By type of crash, head-on collisions were the most often fatal (25.0%).

A fatal share can rank groups quite differently from their number of crashes. Motorcycles were involved in 9,457 of the file's crashes, more than any other type of vehicle except light vehicles, yet only 9.9% of those crashes were fatal, below the average of 12.6%. Heavy vehicles were involved in far fewer (2,717), but 27.8% of them were fatal, more than twice the average.

At night, crashes on roads without street lighting were fatal in 28.1% of cases and those under adequate street lighting in 9.8%, the highest and lowest shares of any lighting condition. Away from a junction, 14.8% of crashes were fatal; inside a junction, 7.7%. The groups overlap: a crash on an unlit interurban road involving a heavy vehicle belongs to several at once, so each fatal share describes the circumstances that go with a fatal outcome without separating the part played by any one of them.

Fatal share by posted speed limit

The speed-limit field records the limit signposted on the road, not how fast anyone was driving; no file in the study records vehicle speeds. In 17,969 crashes (73.4% of the file) no specific limit was posted and the generic limit for the type of road applied. Where a limit was posted, the fatal share rises with it, from 7.4% at 10–30 km/h to 23.9% at 100–120 km/h. Higher limits are posted on faster, mostly interurban roads, so the rise describes the roads as much as the limits.

Crashes by year and province

The number of crashes with a death or serious injury fell from 1,984 in 2010 to 1,208 in 2020, the lowest of the period, and was 1,732 in 2023. Relative to population, such crashes in 2023 were most frequent in the province of Lleida, with 41.8 per 100,000 residents, followed by the provinces of Tarragona (30.6), Girona (22.0) and Barcelona (19.1). These are rates per resident; the file has no measure of how far people travel.

Agreement with DGT's national records

The Catalan file shares no record identifier with DGT's national crash records, so the two are compared by year and province (the file's traffic demarcations). Over 2016–2023, the file's count of fatal crashes equals DGT's count of crashes with a death within 24 hours in 32 of 32 province-years, and its total count matches DGT's 24-hour count of crashes with a death or serious injury to within one crash in every province-year. Its fatal count is 67%–95% of DGT's 30-day count. The file does not state its definition, but the counts show that it treats a crash as fatal when someone died within 24 hours, and every comparison with national figures uses that definition.

Fields recorded unevenly

Eight fields are left blank or marked “not specified” more often in non-fatal crashes than in fatal ones. The field for special lanes, for example, is blank in 7.7% of non-fatal crashes and 1.0% of fatal ones. The file gives no reason, but the blank itself carries information about the outcome, so the Catalonia crash-severity model leaves these fields out. Otherwise the file is consistent: it has no duplicated rows, and every severity label agrees with the deaths and injuries recorded on the same row. The repository's data-quality report documents these checks.

Limitations. The file describes crashes only. It has no records of the people involved and no alcohol or drug test results.
