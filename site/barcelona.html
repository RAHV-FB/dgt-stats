Barcelona publishes six tables for 2025 that share one crash number: the crash itself, its type, the people involved, vehicle records and two lists of recorded causes. Together they cover 7,741 crashes and 17,200 person records, injured or not. Among people with a recorded outcome, 1.6% were seriously or fatally injured: 6.5% of pedestrians and 3.3% of motorcycle drivers, against 0.1% of car drivers. These are shares among people involved in recorded crashes, not risks per trip.

Which crashes

Crashes are most frequent at 14:00 (540 crashes in the year); 3.3% of all crashes had a serious or fatal injury. Counts by hour are frequency: they follow how much traffic there is, which the data do not measure.

Recorded causes

Each cause below was recorded by the police for the crash. A recorded cause is not a finding that it caused the crash, and the driver-cause list has no key to the person or vehicle concerned, so it is never attributed to anyone. Alcohol was recorded as a mediate factor in 337 crashes; 47.5% of them were at night, against 9.3% of the rest.

Two things the files needed

The crash file lists its UTM columns as Y then X, and its X column holds northings: its two labels are exchanged. A UTM easting in Spain is always below 1,000,000 m and a northing above 3,000,000 m, so each value is placed by its size, in new columns beside the untouched source columns. Afterwards every file gives each crash the same position, and the UTM position sits a constant 94 m east and 204 m north of the WGS84 position, the datum shift between ED50 and WGS84. Maps use WGS84.

The vehicle table has 16,536 rows for 14,350 vehicles the crash table reports, with no vehicle identifier. Counting vehicles from it is therefore quarantined; only whether a type of vehicle appears in a crash is used (the types agree with the person records in every crash).

Full results: severity by person group, severity by crash group and recorded cause, crashes by month, hour, weekday and district, alcohol- and speed-recorded crashes, table structure, vehicle-table audit (CSV).

Conclusion

Barcelona's tables are relational: one crash number links the crash, its people and its recorded causes. Pedestrians and motorcyclists are much more often seriously injured than car occupants among the people involved in recorded crashes. Causes are recorded at crash level, and the vehicle table cannot be read as one row per vehicle.

Limits. One year of one city; frequency and severity only (no exposure); person severity excludes records with no recorded outcome and one natural death; no link to Catalonia's file or to DGT records exists or is attempted.
