Crash severity

Crashes and casualties in Barcelona, 2025

The Guàrdia Urbana, Barcelona's city police, recorded 7,741 crashes in 2025: every crash it attended, with every person involved. Of the 15,848 people whose outcome was recorded, 1.6% were seriously injured (more than 24 hours in hospital) or killed. Pedestrians (6.5%) and motorcyclists (3.3%) were far more often seriously hurt than car drivers (3 of 4,671, 0.06%). These are shares of people in recorded crashes, not rates per journey. Spain's figures (deaths within 30 days) and Catalonia's (deaths within 24 hours) cover other crashes.

Pedestrians and people aged 75 and over were most often seriously hurt

The shares for cyclists (1.3%), moped riders (2.2%) and personal mobility vehicle riders (2.1%) lie between those of car drivers and motorcyclists. By age, the share is highest among people aged 75 and over (5.3%); in every younger age band it lies between 0.9% and 2.1%. Women and men differ little (1.8% and 1.6%).

Crashes in which a pedestrian was struck were most often serious

Of the 7,741 crashes, including those in which nobody was hurt, 3.3% had at least one serious or fatal injury. That is more than the share of people, because one seriously injured person places a crash in the group. Among crash types with at least 30 crashes, the share is highest when a pedestrian was struck: 7.3%, more than twice the average.

One crash-type code records the outcome rather than a kind of crash. The Guàrdia Urbana codes rear-end collisions with its own term, but in 43 crashes it used the Catalan file's term for one, and 26 of those had a serious or fatal injury, against 2 of the 1,672 with its own code. The page counts both as rear-end collisions, which had a serious or fatal injury in 1.6% of 1,715 crashes.

Two models were fitted on these records, and neither gives probabilities on this site. The crash model ranked crashes no better than the shares by crash type, which the site gives instead; model and shares alike read the crash type as recorded, the code above included. The person model can only rank people and is kept for research only (how the models were judged).

The police recorded lack of attention to driving more often than any other cause

For most crashes the Guàrdia Urbana records one or more causes or contributing factors, such as lack of attention, alcohol or the state of the road. They are the police's classification after attending the crash, not causal estimates. They are also recorded for the crash as a whole, so a cause cannot be tied to the driver or vehicle concerned, or to the person who was hurt.

Lack of attention to driving was recorded in 2,082 crashes. Among causes recorded in at least 30 crashes, a serious or fatal injury was most often recorded where excessive or inappropriate speed was: 18.4% of 38 crashes (95% interval 9.2–33.4%), against 3.3% of all crashes. Alcohol was recorded in 337 crashes, 47.5% of them at night, against 9.3% of the other crashes. Every recorded cause is listed in the technical notes.

Limitations. The records cover one year in one city, so they show no change over time, and they share no identifier with the Catalan file or DGT's records.
