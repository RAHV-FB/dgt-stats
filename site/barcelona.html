Crash severity

Crashes and casualties in Barcelona, 2025

Every crash Barcelona's city police attended in 2025, with each person involved: who was seriously hurt, in which kinds of crash, and the causes the police recorded.

The Guàrdia Urbana, Barcelona's city police, recorded 7,741 crashes in 2025: every crash it attended, including those in which nobody was hurt, with every person involved. A person counts as seriously injured after more than 24 hours in hospital, and the shares on this page put serious and fatal injuries together. Among the 15,848 people whose outcome was recorded, 1.6% were seriously or fatally injured. Pedestrians and motorcyclists were much more often seriously or fatally injured than car drivers: 6.5% of pedestrians and 3.3% of motorcyclists, against 3 of 4,671 car drivers (0.06%). These shares are among people in recorded crashes, not rates per journey or per kilometre. The pages on crash circumstances in Spain (every injury crash, deaths within 30 days) and Catalonia (crashes with a death or serious injury, deaths within 24 hours) cover other crashes with other definitions, so their figures differ.

Pedestrians and people aged 75 and over were most often seriously hurt

The shares for cyclists (1.3%), moped riders (2.2%) and personal mobility vehicle riders (2.1%) lie between those of car drivers and motorcyclists. By age, the share is highest among people aged 75 and over (5.3%); in every younger age band it lies between 0.9% and 2.1%. Women and men differ little (1.8% and 1.6%). The shares leave out the 1,351 people whose outcome was not recorded and one death the police recorded as natural; counted as uninjured, the people without a recorded outcome would lower the overall share from 1.6% to 1.5%.

Crashes in which a pedestrian was struck were most often serious

Of the 7,741 crashes the police attended, 3.3% had at least one serious or fatal injury, more than the share of people because one seriously injured person is enough to place a crash in that group. The police recorded no victim in 890 of them; among the crashes with a victim the share is 3.7%. Among crash types with at least 30 crashes, the share is highest when a pedestrian was struck: 7.3%, more than twice the average. Rear-end collisions had a serious or fatal injury in 1.6% of 1,715 crashes. They include the crashes coded with the Catalan file's term for a rear-end collision: 26 of those 43 crashes had a serious or fatal injury, against 2 of the 1,865 under the Guàrdia Urbana's own rear-end codes. The term is used when a crash is serious, so it records the outcome rather than a kind of crash, and this page counts it with the other rear-end collisions. A model of these crashes ranked them no better than a table of shares by accident type; both were fitted on the crash type as recorded, the term above included (how the models were judged).

Causes recorded by the police

For most crashes the Guàrdia Urbana records one or more causes or contributing factors, such as lack of attention, alcohol or the state of the road. No driver cause is recorded for 436 crashes and only “not determined” for 631; contributing factors are recorded for 473. They are classifications the police make after attending the crash, a crash can have several, and they are not causal estimates produced by this study. They are also recorded for the crash as a whole: no key links a cause to the driver or vehicle concerned, so a crash with alcohol recorded cannot be tied to the person who had been drinking, or to the person who was hurt.

The cause recorded most often is lack of attention to driving (2,082 crashes). Among causes recorded in at least 30 crashes, a serious or fatal injury was most often recorded where excessive or inappropriate speed was: 18.4% of 38 crashes (95% interval 9.2–33.4%), against 3.3% of all crashes. Alcohol was recorded in 337 crashes, 47.5% of them at night, against 9.3% of the other crashes.
