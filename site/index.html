Road safety in Spain

An independent statistical study of road deaths and injuries in Spain from 1993 to 2024, built from official statistics and from police crash records for Catalonia and Barcelona.

Most of this study is ordinary statistical analysis of published data: deaths and crashes counted over time and divided by residents, licence holders, vehicles, fuel sold or kilometres driven, and, where the data allow, split into how often crashes happen and how deadly a crash is once it has happened. A smaller part fits predictive models to individual crash records from Catalonia and Barcelona, and keeps a model only if it beats a simple table of the same records. Every comparison between an outcome and a circumstance is an association, not an estimate of a causal effect.

Main findings

  1. −74%

    Road deaths in Spain fell from 6,378 in 1993 to 1,680 in 2013, most of the fall coming between 2003 and 2013. That steep decline then ended: the trend since 2013 shows no clear rise or fall, and the 1,785 deaths of 2024 were more than in 2013. Since 2019, no measure of deaths, whether counted or divided by residents, licence holders, vehicles or fuel sold, has changed by more than ordinary year-to-year variation.

    Long-run trends · Trends since 2019

  2. −73%

    Deaths per injury crash fell 73% between 1996 and 2024, while injury crashes per tonne of road fuel sold fell 13%. Deaths relative to traffic fell 76% over the period, mostly because crashes became less deadly rather than less frequent. How the fall divides between the two depends on how completely crashes with only slight injuries are recorded.

    Crash frequency and severity

  3. 3.9×

    In 2024, car drivers aged 75 and over who were involved in an injury crash died 3.9 times as often as drivers aged 35–54 (15.9 against 4.1 per 1,000 involved), a result that needs no estimate of kilometres.

    Drivers: deaths once a crash has happened

  4. 2.0–6.8×

    On DGT's kilometre estimates, which are published by the age of a car's registered owner and not its driver, drivers aged 18–24 were involved in 6.8 times as many injury crashes per kilometre as drivers aged 35–54. Under a deliberately extreme reassignment of kilometres to young owners, the ratio falls to 2.0, still above 1. Once involved, they died about as often as drivers aged 35–54.

    Drivers: crashes per kilometre

  5. 2.0×

    In Spain outside Catalonia and the Basque Country, police recorded inappropriate speed in 6.9% of injury crashes in 2023. Those crashes had about twice the deaths per crash of other crashes on the same kind of road in the same year. This is an association in police records, not an estimate of how many crashes or deaths speeding caused.

    Speed · Recorded factors

  6. 2 of 3

    Of the three models that rank recorded crashes, or the people in them, by severity in Catalonia and Barcelona, two did better on later records than a simple table of the same records. The third did no better than a table, and the table is reported instead. A version of the Catalan model ranked serious crashes in the rest of Spain almost as well as a model trained on them, which does not make it fit for national use. None of the models predicts whether a crash will happen.

Sources and methodology

Where the data come from, what they cover and how the results are produced.

  • Data sources and scope
  • Methodology

Data: the Dirección General de Tráfico (DGT) yearbook series 1993–2024, its tables of drivers and vehicles involved in crashes, its file of injury crashes since 2016 and its estimates of kilometres driven; INE population figures, road-fuel sales and traffic counts; Catalonia's crashes with a death or serious injury, 2010–2023; and Barcelona's police-attended crashes, 2025. The sources share no record identifier and are analysed separately. Data sources and scope.

Road safety in Spain, an independent analysis by Russell Howard (RAHV-FB). Data from the Dirección General de Tráfico, INE, the Ministerio de Transportes, CORES, the Servei Català de Trànsit and the Ajuntament de Barcelona. All results are computed from the published files by the code in the repository.
