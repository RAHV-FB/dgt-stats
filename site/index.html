Where to start

Three of the nine analyses, chosen because between them they show what these sources carry and where they stop: a model with its validation, a measurement that changes answer with its denominator, and a case where the checks did not support the claim.

What makes a crash fatal

Given an injury crash, the crash type 'pedestrian struck' carries 6.4 times the odds of a death of the reference type; a model fitted to 2016–2022 scores 2023–2024 with an area under the curve of 0.80.

Two logistic regressions on every injury crash since 2016, with province-clustered intervals, average marginal effects, a 2023–2024 holdout and a refit for every year.

Whether older drivers are riskier depends on the denominator

Drivers aged 75 and over die 0.74 times as often as drivers aged 35–64 per resident, 1.55 times per licence holder and 2.95 times per driver involved in a crash: the denominator decides the answer.

The same driver deaths divided by residents, by licence holders, by a travel-weighted estimate of who actually drives, and by drivers involved in crashes.

Two policy changes, one claim withheld

The July 2006 points licence coincided with a 12% drop in monthly deaths beyond the trend, larger than any of the 38 placebo breaks (rank 1); the 2019 speed limit shows a change relative to the control roads, but a placebo break in January 2017 gives one of its own (+12%), so no claim is made.

Segmented Poisson regressions with placebo breaks, a control group of roads and sensitivity fits; the wording follows what those checks support, not the headline.

The other six analyses

  • Trends: 1,785 deaths in 2024, 72% fewer than in 1993; the fall stopped around 2013.
  • Timing: Crashes in darkness (lighting recorded as dark) are 25% of interurban injury crashes but 34% of interurban deaths (2024).
  • Road users: Pedestrians, cyclists, moped riders, motorcyclists and personal-mobility-vehicle users are 79% of urban deaths and 40% of interurban ones (2024).
  • Geography: Deaths per 100,000 residents run from 1.2 in Melilla to 14.5 in Zamora (2024), with intervals wide enough that most provinces overlap.
  • Vehicles per km: A heavy truck is in a fatal crash 10 times as often as a car per circulating vehicle but 2.5 times per kilometre driven (2022); most of the people killed are outside the truck.
  • Speed: DGT's report records inappropriate speed in 7% of injury crashes (2023, without Cataluña and País Vasco); in the yearbook's driver tables 52% of drivers have no speed record at all.

How to read the numbers

Counts are DGT's consolidated figures: an injury crash is one with at least one person killed or injured, and deaths are counted within 30 days of the crash unless a chart says otherwise. Crash counts describe what the police recorded, not the risk of travelling; rates on the trends page divide by the vehicle fleet or the population.

About this project

An independent analysis of Spanish road-safety open data by RAHV-FB. The questions, the choice of sources, the statistical design and the reading of the results are the author's, as is responsibility for what is published here; the methods are set out in the methodology note, and every number on these pages is regenerated from the raw files by the scripts in the repository, written with the help of AI coding assistants. The code is under the MIT licence; the data keep the terms of the bodies that publish them.

An independent analysis by RAHV-FB, built from Dirección General de Tráfico (DGT) open data (crash microdata 2016–2024, the Anuario de Accidentes 2024 series, statistical tables 2014–2024, the driver census 2014–2025, the ITV kilometre estimates 2022 and the ONSV speed-factor report), INE resident population 2002–2025 (CC BY 4.0) and the ESRA and MOVILIA surveys; sources and licences are on the data page. Every number is reproducible from the repository; see the data page for the checks.
