Road safety in Spain

An independent statistical study of road deaths and injuries in Spain from 1993 to 2024, built from official statistics and from police crash records for Catalonia and Barcelona.

Most of this study is ordinary statistical analysis of published data: deaths and crashes counted over time and divided by residents, licence holders, vehicles, fuel sold or kilometres driven, and, where the data allow, split into how often crashes happen and how deadly a crash is once it has happened. A smaller part fits predictive models to individual crash records from Catalonia and Barcelona, and keeps a model only if it beats a simple table of the same records. Every comparison between an outcome and a circumstance is an association, not an estimate of a causal effect.

Main findings

  1. −74%

    Road deaths in Spain fell from 6,378 in 1993 to 1,680 in 2013, most of the fall coming between 2003 and 2013. That steep decline then ended: the trend since 2013 shows no clear rise or fall, and the 1,785 deaths of 2024 were more than in 2013. Between 2019 and 2024, no measure of deaths, whether counted or divided by residents, licence holders, vehicles or fuel sold, changed by more than ordinary year-to-year variation.

    Long-run trends · Trends since 2019

  2. −76%

    Deaths per tonne of road fuel sold, which stands in for traffic, fell 76% between 1996 and 2024. How the fall divides between fewer crashes and less deadly ones cannot be told from the published series. Counted by injury crashes, deaths per crash fell 73% and crashes per tonne 13%; counted by people admitted to hospital, deaths per admission rose 15% and admissions per tonne fell 79%. Each split depends on how completely the less serious casualties were recorded.

    Crash frequency and severity

  3. 3.4×

    In 2024, car drivers aged 75 and over who were involved in an injury crash died 3.4 times as often as drivers aged 45–64 (15.9 against 4.6 per 1,000 involved), a result that needs no estimate of kilometres.

    Drivers: deaths once a crash has happened

  4. 2.5×

    Per kilometre driven, car drivers aged 18–29 were involved in injury crashes about 2.5 times as often as drivers aged 45–64 in 2024 (95% interval 2.3–2.8; 1.6–3.6 under other assumptions about the kilometres), and drivers aged 65 and over 1.19 times as often (0.99–1.65). The kilometres by driver age are estimated from the Barcelona-area working-day travel survey applied to Spain's population.

    Drivers: crashes per kilometre

  5. 2.0×

    In Spain outside Catalonia and the Basque Country, police recorded inappropriate speed in 6.9% of injury crashes in 2023. Over 2016–2023, those crashes had more deaths per crash than other crashes on the same kind of road, from 1.2 times on dual carriageways to 5.9 times on urban streets, and about twice as many (2.0 times) with the road types taken together. This is an association in police records, not an estimate of how many crashes or deaths speeding caused.

    Speed · Recorded factors

  6. 30% / 3%

    Among crashes in Catalonia in which someone was killed or seriously injured, a model of the recorded road, conditions and crash sorted the crashes of 2016–2023, each year predicted from earlier years only, into more and less deadly groups: of the fifth it rated most likely to be fatal, 30% were; of the fifth it rated least likely, 3%. A table of fatal shares by road and crash type separates them less (27% and 4%). A calculator applies the model to a crash a reader describes. It cannot say whether a crash will happen, and it has been tested only within Catalonia.

Sources and methodology

Where the data come from, what they cover and how the results are produced.

  • Data sources and scope
  • Methodology

Data: the Dirección General de Tráfico (DGT) yearbook series 1993–2024, its tables of drivers and vehicles involved in crashes, its file of injury crashes since 2016 and its estimates of kilometres driven; INE population figures, road-fuel sales and traffic counts; Catalonia's crashes with a death or serious injury, 2010–2023; and Barcelona's police-attended crashes, 2025; and two travel surveys for kilometres by driver age, the Barcelona area's working-day mobility survey (EMEF) and Madrid's household travel survey of 2018. The sources share no record identifier and are analysed separately. Data sources and scope.

Road safety in Spain, an independent analysis by Russell Howard (RAHV-FB). Data from the Dirección General de Tráfico, INE, the Ministerio de Transportes, CORES, the Servei Català de Trànsit, the Ajuntament de Barcelona, the Autoritat del Transport Metropolità (EMEF) and the Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid (Powered by CRTM). All results are computed from the published files by the code in the repository.
