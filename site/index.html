Road safety in Spain

An independent statistical study of road deaths and injuries in Spain from 1993 to 2024, built from official statistics and from police crash records for Catalonia and for Barcelona (2025).

Road deaths in Spain fell by about three quarters between 1993 and 2013 and have not fallen since: 1,785 people died in 2024. The findings below come from official statistics and police crash records. Every comparison between an outcome and a circumstance is an association in those records, not an estimate of what caused it.

Main findings

  • Deaths fell steeply until 2013 and have levelled off since. Road deaths fell from 6,378 in 1993 to 1,680 in 2013, most of the fall coming between 2003 and 2013; since 2013 the trend shows no clear rise or fall, and 1,785 people died in 2024. Against 2019, the count of deaths and every death rate changed by no more than ordinary year-to-year variation. Against the falling trend of the 2010s, deaths per tonne of road fuel in 2023 and 2024 were higher than projected, by 8% to 16% depending on where that trend is taken to start; with the trend started in 2013, neither year lies outside the trend's range, and on interurban roads deaths per measured kilometre stayed within theirs.

    Long-run trends · Recent years against the trend · Since 2019

  • Deaths relative to traffic fell by three quarters, but the records cannot say how much of that came from fewer crashes and how much from less deadly ones. Deaths per tonne of road fuel sold, which stands in for traffic, fell 76% between 1996 and 2024. Counted by injury crashes, deaths per crash fell 73% and crashes per tonne 13%; counted by people admitted to hospital, deaths per admission rose 15% and admissions per tonne fell 79%.

    Crash frequency and severity

  • Older drivers are far more likely to die once a crash has happened. In 2024, car drivers aged 75 and over who were involved in an injury crash died 3.4 times as often as drivers aged 45–64 (15.9 against 4.6 per 1,000 involved), a count that needs no estimate of kilometres. It measures how often a crash kills the driver, not how often older drivers are in crashes or who caused them.

    Drivers: deaths once a crash has happened

  • Young drivers are in more crashes for the distance they drive. Per kilometre driven, car drivers aged 18–29 were involved in injury crashes 2.53 times as often as drivers aged 45–64 in 2024 (95% sampling interval 2.2–2.8; sensitivity range 1.49–3.63). For drivers aged 65 and over the central estimate is 1.19 times (95% sampling interval 1.0–1.4), but the sensitivity range is 0.85–1.82, so whether they are involved more or less often per kilometre is not established. For drivers aged 75 and over no source measures their kilometres apart from those at 65–74; depending on how the 65-and-over kilometres are divided and carried to Spain, the sensitivity range is 0.97 to 3.20 times the 45–64 rate, so the data do not establish that they are involved more often per kilometre than drivers aged 45–64, or by how much. If people aged 75 and over drive as much less than those aged 65–74 as in Madrid in 2018, they were involved about 2.1 times as often (95% sampling interval 1.6–2.6), a conditional estimate that holds only on that assumption. The kilometres by driver age come from a Barcelona-area survey of working days. Carried to Spain's population, the survey's working-day driving adds up to about half of DGT's car kilometres; the central estimate gives the rest the same age mix, and the sensitivity range tests other mixes. Involvement counts every driver in a crash, whoever caused it.

    Drivers: crashes per kilometre · Drivers: ages 75 and over

  • Crashes in which the police recorded inappropriate speed are deadlier, most of all on urban streets. In Spain outside Catalonia and the Basque Country, police recorded inappropriate speed in 6.9% of injury crashes in 2023. Over 2016–2023, those crashes had more deaths per crash than other crashes on the same kind of road, from 1.2 times on dual carriageways to 5.9 times on urban streets, and about twice as many (2.0 times) with the road types taken together.

    Speed · Recorded factors

  • In Catalonia, severe crashes with a heavy vehicle involved were fatal about twice as often. Among crashes in Catalonia in which someone was killed or seriously injured, the same crash on a regional road was fatal 2.0 times as often with a lorry or bus involved (95% interval 1.7–2.2), and on an urban street rather than a regional road 0.27 times as often (95% interval 0.20–0.37), other recorded circumstances held equal. A calculator gives the model's estimate for a crash a reader describes. The way the model is fitted and tuned has been tested only within Catalonia, by predicting each year from the years before it; the published model, fitted on every year, has no later year left to test.

The pages

Explore

  • Interactive tools: calculators and explorers that compute results from the site's data.
  • Crash severity calculator: the estimated fatal share of a severe crash in Catalonia, for a crash you describe.
  • Driver risk comparison: crash involvement and deaths of drivers of two age groups or sexes, side by side.
  • Crash statistics explorer: spain's recorded injury crashes and deaths by year, region, road and crash type.
  • Trends and rates explorer: road deaths, injuries and crashes since 1993, as counts and as rates.

Over time

  • Long-run trends: how road deaths have changed since 1993, against vehicles, fuel sold and kilometres driven.
  • Since 2019: deaths, hospital admissions and injury crashes in 2024 against 2019.
  • Seasons: which months are deadliest, and how the 2020 lockdown changed them.
  • The 2006 points licence: whether the points-based licence of July 2006 changed monthly deaths.

Drivers, vehicles and factors

  • Drivers: how often drivers of each age and sex are in crashes per kilometre, and how often a crash kills them.
  • Vehicles: crashes and deaths by type of vehicle, per vehicle and per kilometre.
  • Speed: crashes in which the police recorded inappropriate speed, and their deaths.
  • Recorded factors: the other circumstances the police record, and how their shares have moved.

Crash severity

  • Crash circumstances in Spain: which recorded circumstances go with a death in Spain's injury crashes.
  • Catalonia: crashes with a death or serious injury in Catalonia, 2010–2023.
  • Barcelona: every crash the Guàrdia Urbana attended in Barcelona in 2025.
  • Severity model and calculator: a model of which severe crashes in Catalonia were fatal, and a calculator to try it.
  • External validation: how the Catalan models held up on other years, places and records.
