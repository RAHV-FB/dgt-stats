Spain publishes a great deal of road-safety data and very little analysis of it. These four pages each take a question the published tables leave open, and follow it until the evidence either holds or breaks. Two of the four answers came out weaker than they first looked, and both pages say so.

Finding 1Dangerous-looking conditions go with less severe crashes

Once an injury crash has happened, a wet road carries 0.56× the odds of a death of a dry one, and a junction 0.75×. Dropping either of the two correlated predictors leaves the result standing, and so does fitting urban and interurban roads separately. This is about how badly a crash ends, not about how often one happens.

Two logistic models of all 875,013 injury crashes since 2016, with province-clustered intervals, a 2023 to 2024 holdout (AUC 0.80) and eight sensitivity fits.

Finding 2Older drivers crash no more per kilometre; they die far more often when they do

Car drivers aged 75 and over are involved in injury crashes 1.02× as often per kilometre driven as drivers aged 35 to 54, which is to say about as often. Once involved they are killed 3.93× as often. Their death rate per kilometre is 3.99× the middle-aged rate, and nearly all of that comes from the second figure rather than the first.

Car-driver deaths and involvements from DGT's driver tables, divided by DGT's 2024 estimate of kilometres driven by owner age band. The baseline is built the same way as the older groups.

Finding 3A heavy truck is ten times a car, or two and a half times, depending on the divisor

Per circulating vehicle a heavy truck is in a fatal crash 10.3 times as often as a car. Per kilometre driven it is 2.5 times. For every fatal crash a truck is in, 0.18 of its own occupants die, against 0.93 for a motorcycle, so most of the danger a truck carries is to other people.

DGT's 2022 kilometre estimates divided into the same year's involvement counts, with exact Poisson intervals.

Finding 4A twelve per cent policy effect falls to about seven under the right test

Monthly deaths stepped down at July 2006, when the points licence came in. A straight pre-trend puts the step at −12%. The pre-trend the earlier months actually prefer puts it at −7%. Placed at July of other years the same model ranks 2006 first of 15, but a forecast made before each July finds 2006 only the fourth most abnormal of them.

Segmented Poisson regression on monthly deaths, 1993 to 2024, with calendar-matched placebos, seasonality-free summer transitions, out-of-sample forecasts and two monthly traffic series as exposure.

A warning about one published series

The share of drivers in an injury crash with no recorded speed status went from 17% in 2014 to 52% in 2016 and has stayed there. Any trend read off DGT's published speed-infraction column crosses that break. The context page shows what it does to the two obvious readings of the same table.

How to read the numbers

Counts are DGT's consolidated figures. An injury crash is one with at least one person killed or injured, and deaths are counted within 30 days. Every rate names its denominator, because the denominator is usually where the answer comes from. Model results are associations. The 2006 page says coincided with rather than caused, because its falsification tests do not support the stronger word. Sources, definitions and the 434 reconciliation checks are on the data page.
