Drivers, vehicles and factors

Police-recorded inappropriate speed and crash severity

In Spain outside Catalonia and the Basque Country, police recorded inappropriate speed in 6.9% of injury crashes in 2023, and those crashes accounted for 21.8% of the deaths. Over 2016–2023, crashes with speed recorded had 3.41 times as many deaths per crash as other injury crashes. Part of that gap reflects where they happen: they are concentrated on interurban roads other than motorways and dual carriageways, where any crash is more often fatal. Compared with other crashes on the same kind of road, they had more deaths per crash on every road type, from 1.22 times on dual carriageways to 5.90 times on urban streets; the weighted summary across road types, with the year allowed for, is 2.00 times (95% interval 1.69–2.36). These are associations in police records, not estimates of what speed causes.

The ratio was largest on urban streets, where an ordinary injury crash rarely kills (0.73 deaths per 100 crashes, against 4.32 with speed recorded). On interurban roads other than motorways and dual carriageways it was 1.75, and on motorways 1.90. The all-roads summary compares crashes within the same road type and year and is weighted towards other interurban roads, where most deaths in crashes with speed recorded occur. The road-type ratios differ by more than chance, so the summary describes no single kind of road, and its interval is widened to allow for how much they differ.

How the speed report and the crash records are combined

DGT's speed report, the Informe temático Factor Velocidad, leaves out Catalonia and the Basque Country, which keep their own crash records. It gives crashes and deaths with speed recorded by road type, but totals of all crashes only for all roads, interurban roads and urban streets. DGT's crash records for the same provinces reproduce those three totals exactly in every year from 2016 to 2023, and supply the totals for each interurban road type. The report's interurban categories are matched to the road types of the crash records by name (autopistas to motorways, autovías to dual carriageways, the rest to other interurban roads); that match is checked only through the interurban total, not road type by road type.

The adjusted ratio comes from a quasi-Poisson model of deaths per crash with road type and year. It brings the crude ratio of 3.41 down to 2.00; on a logarithmic scale, road type and year account for 43% of the crude ratio. The model assumes one ratio for every road type. Letting the ratio differ by road type improves the fit by a likelihood-ratio statistic of 446 on 3 degrees of freedom and lowers the Pearson dispersion from 13.5 to 2.0: most of the extra variation the summary's interval allows for is the difference between road types. The road-type intervals are Poisson intervals widened by the square root of each road type's own dispersion across years (1.9 to 2.4).

The ratio describes police records, not measured speeds

Inappropriate speed is a recorded factor: a judgement the police record about a crash, usually once its outcome is known (Recorded factors explains these records). No file records how fast anyone was travelling. The ratio shows that crashes with speed recorded were deadlier than other crashes on the same kind of road. It does not show that speed caused those crashes or the deaths in them, and it does not separate speed from other factors recorded in the same crash. Recording could bias the ratio either way: police may look harder for speed when someone has died, which would raise it, while speed that played a part but went unrecorded leaves those crashes among the others, which would lower it. The data cannot measure either bias.

The driver tables cannot show a trend in speeding

DGT's yearbook tables cover all of Spain, including Catalonia and the Basque Country, and record for each driver in an injury crash whether the police noted a speed infraction. From 2016, about half of drivers have no speed status recorded (52% in 2016 and 52% in 2024, against 17% in 2014). The two obvious readings of the tables therefore point in opposite directions: the share of all drivers with a speed infraction fell from 6.7% in 2014 to 4.3% in 2024, while the share among drivers with a recorded status rose from 8.1% to 9.1%. The speed report's crash series, used above, has no break of this kind; Recorded factors follows its trend.
