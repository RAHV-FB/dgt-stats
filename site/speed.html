Spain

Recorded speed and crash severity

Deaths per injury crash when the police recorded inappropriate speed, compared with other crashes on the same kind of road, and a change in how DGT records speed infractions.

In Spain outside Catalonia and the Basque Country, injury crashes in which the police recorded inappropriate speed were 6.9% of all injury crashes in 2023 and 21.8% of the deaths. Over 2016–2023 they killed 3.41× as many people per crash as other injury crashes. Part of that difference reflects location: such crashes concentrate on interurban roads other than motorways and dual carriageways, where any injury crash is more often fatal. Compared with other crashes on the same kind of road in the same year, a crash with speed recorded had about twice as many deaths: 2.00× (1.69–2.36). A second source on speed, DGT's tables of drivers involved in injury crashes, changed in 2016: from that year about half the drivers have no speed status recorded.

On every road type, crashes with speed recorded were deadlier than other crashes, and every interval lies above one. The size of the difference varies widely. On urban streets, where an ordinary injury crash rarely kills (0.73 deaths per 100), a crash with speed recorded had 5.90× as many deaths; on other interurban roads the ratio was 1.75×, and on dual carriageways 1.22×. The adjusted ratio summarises this variation in one figure, and its interval is widened to allow for it.

How the speed report and the crash records are combined

DGT's speed report, the Informe temático Factor Velocidad, leaves out Catalonia and the Basque Country, which keep their own crash records. It gives crashes and deaths with speed recorded by road type, but totals of all crashes only for all roads, interurban roads and urban streets. DGT's crash records restricted to the same provinces reproduce those three totals exactly in every year from 2016 to 2023, the years both sources cover, and they supply the totals for each interurban road type. Within interurban roads the report's categories are matched to the road types of the crash records by name (autopistas to motorways, autovías to dual carriageways, the rest to other interurban roads). That match is checked only through the interurban total and is not reconciled road type by road type: a mismatch would move crashes between the three interurban rows, and the ratios by interurban road type and the adjusted ratio depend on it.

The adjusted ratio comes from a quasi-Poisson model of deaths per crash with road type and year. It brings the crude ratio of 3.41× down to 2.00×; measured on a logarithmic scale, which splits a ratio into additive parts, road type and year account for 43% of the crude ratio.

A record written after the crash

Inappropriate speed in these data is a judgement the police record after the crash, once the outcome is known. Two biases follow from that timing, and they pull in opposite directions. Fatal crashes may be investigated more thoroughly, so speed may be recorded more often when someone has died, which would inflate the ratio. Speed that played a part but went unrecorded leaves those crashes in the comparison group, which would deflate it. The tables measure neither effect, so their balance is unknown.

The ratio is therefore an association in the police record and not an estimate of how many deaths speed caused. That estimate, like the effect of a change in speeds, would require measured travelling speeds, and the files record speed only as the police's judgement.

A change in the speed-infraction record in 2016

DGT's yearbook tables also record, for each driver involved in an injury crash, whether the police noted a speed infraction. In 2014, 17% of drivers had no speed status recorded. In 2016 the share jumped to 52%, and it has stayed close to half since (52% in 2024). As a result, the two obvious readings of the table point in opposite directions. The share of all drivers with a speed infraction fell from 6.7% to 4.3% between 2014 and 2024, while the share among drivers with a recorded status rose from 8.1% to 9.1%. With half the drivers unclassified, neither series measures a trend in speeding. The speed report's series of crashes with speed recorded, used above, shows no break of this kind (Recorded factors tests every factor series for breaks).

Limitations. A crash may carry several recorded factors, and the ratio does not separate speed from the others recorded with it.
