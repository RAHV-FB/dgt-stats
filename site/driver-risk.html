Driver risk comparison

Car drivers of two age groups, or men and women, side by side: involvement in injury crashes and deaths per licence holder, per driver involved and per kilometre driven.

Compare
Groups
Assumptions about distance driven

This tool needs JavaScript. The results it draws on are on the pages linked below.

Involvement counts every driver in an injury crash, whoever caused it: it does not measure responsibility, which the public records do not hold. Deaths per driver involved say how often a crash kills the driver, not how often drivers crash.

Rates per licence holder and per driver involved are observed counts, with 95% intervals for chance variation. Rates per kilometre are estimates: the kilometres driven by each age come from travel surveys, so each figure carries a 95% sampling interval and a sensitivity range, the span of every assumption tested; the range, not one assumption, says what the data establish. The assumptions are set out on the drivers page and the methodology page.

About this tool
Question
How often private-car drivers of one age group, or men, are involved in injury crashes or killed in them, compared with another group or with women.
Population
Drivers of private cars in Spain, 2024; taxi and ride-hailing drivers are left out.
Unit
The driver involved in an injury crash.
Source
DGT's crash records and yearbook tables (drivers involved and killed); DGT's licence register; DGT's car kilometres from inspection odometer readings, shared out by age with the Barcelona (EMEF) and Madrid (2018) travel surveys.
What you can choose
Age groups or sex, the measure, and for the per-kilometre estimates the age profile of the kilometres and how the kilometres of people aged 65 and over are split at 75.
Outcome
Drivers involved in an injury crash; drivers killed.
Denominator
B-licence holders, kilometres driven (estimated), or drivers involved.
Kind of result
Rates per licence holder and per driver involved are observed, with 95% intervals for chance variation; rates per kilometre are estimates that depend on assumptions, with a 95% sampling interval and a sensitivity range kept apart.
Limitations
Involvement counts every driver in a crash, whoever caused it; it is not responsibility. No source measures kilometres by driver age. The figure for 75 and over holds only under the chosen split of the older kilometres and is not established across the assumptions tested.
How it is checked
The test suite recomputes every rate, interval and range from the result tables, and the browser tests compare what the page shows with them.

Road safety in Spain, an independent analysis by Russell Howard (RAHV-FB). Data from the Dirección General de Tráfico, INE, the Ministerio de Transportes, CORES, the Servei Català de Trànsit, the Ajuntament de Barcelona, the Autoritat del Transport Metropolità, Idescat and Institut Metròpoli (EMEF), Idescat (population census) and the Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid (Powered by CRTM). All results are computed by the code in the repository from the published files and the cited values it holds.
