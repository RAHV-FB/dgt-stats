Drivers, vehicles and factors

Driver age and sex

Per kilometre driven, car drivers aged 18–29 in Spain in 2024 were involved in injury crashes an estimated 2.68 times as often as drivers aged 45–64. Once in a crash, drivers aged 75 and over died 3.4 times as often as those aged 45–64, and men 2.6 times as often as women. Involvement counts every driver in a crash, whoever caused it.

Crashes per kilometre: highest among drivers aged 18–29

No source counts kilometres by the driver's age in Spain, so they are estimated: DGT's car kilometres are shared out by age with the average of two travel surveys' age profiles, the Barcelona area's (2022–2024) and Madrid's (2018) (method).

Per kilometre driven, car drivers aged 18–29 were involved in injury crashes 2.68 times as often as drivers aged 45–64 (95% sampling interval 2.5–2.9), drivers aged 30–44 1.24 times (1.18–1.31) and drivers aged 65–74 1.11 times (1.0–1.2).

Drivers aged 75 and over were involved 2.43 times as often (95% sampling interval 2.0–3.0), the least certain of these figures: no survey of the Barcelona area separates their driving from that of people aged 65–74, so it rests on Madrid's survey alone, and under other plausible assumptions it runs from 0.97 to 3.20 times the 45–64 rate, so the data do not establish that they are involved more often per kilometre than drivers aged 45–64, or by how much (why). Madrid survey data: Powered by CRTM.

Deaths once a crash has happened: highest at 75 and over

Counted directly, with no kilometres, 15.9 of every 1,000 car drivers aged 75 and over involved in an injury crash in 2024 died within 30 days, and 8.5 at 65–74, against 4.6 at 45–64. The rate counts only the driver's own death, so it does not show whether older drivers' crashes are more dangerous to others; like involvement, it does not show who caused a crash.

Men and women: men die far more often

Per kilometre, estimated in the same way, male car drivers were involved in injury crashes 0.91 times as often as female drivers (95% sampling interval 0.87–0.95), a gap small enough that other assumptions about who drives how far could reverse it, and they were killed 2.63 times as often (2.1–3.4). Per licence holder, pooling 2022–2024, men were involved 1.39 times as often as women (1.38–1.40), which on the estimated kilometres comes from their driving further; once involved, they died 2.62 times as often (2.28–3.00), in every age band (Figure 3).

Limitations. The kilometres by age and sex share out DGT's kilometre totals, which DGT describes as valid only in aggregate. The crash counts include foreign and unlicensed drivers, and drivers whose age is not recorded (2.3% of those involved) are left out of every rate.
