Drivers and vehicles

Driver age and sex

Car drivers aged 75 and over who are in an injury crash die 3.4 times as often as drivers aged 45–64 (95% interval 2.6–4.5); this is counted from the crash records and needs no estimate of distance. Per kilometre driven, drivers aged 18–29 are involved in injury crashes 2.7 times as often as drivers aged 45–64 (95% sampling interval 2.5–2.9; range under other assumptions 1.5–3.6), and drivers aged 30–44 1.2 times as often (95% sampling interval 1.2–1.3; range 1.1–1.7). Drivers aged 65–74 cannot be told apart from drivers aged 45–64 (1.1 times; interval 1.0–1.2; range 0.7–1.6), and whether drivers aged 75 and over differ is not established (2.4 times; interval 2.0–3.0; range 0.9–3.1). Involvement counts every driver in a crash, whoever caused it.

Crashes per kilometre: clearly higher at 18–29 than at 45–64

No source counts kilometres by the driver's age in Spain, so they are estimated: DGT's car kilometres are shared out by age with the average of two travel surveys' age profiles, the Barcelona area's (2022–2024) and Madrid's (2018) (method). The direction of the differences is robust; their size depends on assumptions, so each ratio is given with its 95% sampling interval and the range under other assumptions: the lowest and highest ratio when another region's profile stands for Spain, the kilometres the survey does not cover are given another age mix, the 65-and-over kilometres are split another way, or the drivers of unrecorded age are placed in one age group.

Deaths once a crash has happened: highest at 75 and over

Counted directly, with no kilometres, 15.9 of every 1,000 car drivers aged 75 and over involved in an injury crash in 2024 died within 30 days, and 8.5 at 65–74, against 4.6 at 45–64: 3.4 times the 45–64 rate at 75 and over (95% interval 2.6–4.5) and 1.8 times at 65–74 (1.4–2.5). The rate counts only the driver's own death, so it does not show whether older drivers' crashes are more dangerous to others; like involvement, it does not show who caused a crash.

Men and women: men die far more often

Per kilometre, estimated in the same way, male car drivers were involved in injury crashes 0.91 times as often as female drivers (95% sampling interval 0.87–0.95; range 0.60–1.23), so neither sex is shown to be involved more often for the distance driven, and they were killed 2.6 times as often (2.1–3.4; range 1.7–3.6). Per licence holder, pooling 2022–2024, men were involved 1.39 times as often as women (1.38–1.40), which on the estimated kilometres comes from their driving further; once involved, they died 2.62 times as often (2.28–3.00), in every age band (Figure 3).

Limitations. The kilometres by age and sex share out DGT's kilometre totals, which DGT describes as valid only in aggregate. The crash counts include foreign and unlicensed drivers, and drivers whose age is not recorded (2.3% of those involved) are left out of every rate.
