Among car drivers in injury crashes in 2024, the share killed within 30 days rose with each age band after 35–54: drivers aged 75 and over were killed 3.93× (2.98–5.18) as often per driver involved as drivers aged 35 to 54, and drivers aged 18 to 34 were killed 0.95× (0.75–1.20) as often. That ratio compares the same drivers, by the same age, in the same tables, and needs no exposure. For distance the repository holds only the kilometres driven by cars registered to owners in each age band, not the kilometres driven by the drivers in it, so every per-km ratio is a range, from a scenario that moves young drivers' kilometres out of the 35–54 band to the published ratio. On that denominator drivers aged 75 and over were killed 3.48–3.99× as often as drivers aged 35 to 54, and drivers aged 18 to 34 were involved in injury crashes 1.55–2.57× as often. Male car drivers were killed 3.63× as often per licence holder as female ones: 1.41× the involvement and 2.57× the deaths per driver involved.

Deaths per driver involved: the measure that needs no kilometres

Against drivers aged 35 to 54, the share of car drivers in an injury crash who were killed rises with every band after it: 1.32× at 55–64, 2.07× at 65–74 and 3.93× at 75 and over. Drivers aged 18 to 34 are at 0.95× (0.75–1.20), an interval that includes no difference. Numerator and denominator are the same drivers, counted by the same age band in DGT's tables 4.1.1 and 4.2, so the kilometres and their owner-age problem do not enter. The measure counts only the driver's own death; DGT's tables do not say how often the other people in those crashes died, and they do not say why the share rises with age.

Per kilometre: kilometres by the owner's age, not the driver's

DGT's 2024 kilometre release estimates how far the circulating fleet is driven, by the age band of the registered owner. The repository's own tables show where owner and driver part. Per holder of a B permit, the car licence, the 18–34 band has 0.46 cars registered to it and 6,247 km a year, against 0.80 cars and 10,345 km at 35–54: either young licence holders drive less, or part of their driving is in cars registered to older owners, and the data cannot tell which. The 75-and-over band has 1.14 cars per B-permit holder, more cars than people of that age licensed to drive them: either some of those cars are driven by others, or some drivers have more than one, and again the data cannot tell which.

A transfer scenario puts a number on the first gap: kilometres move from the 35–54 band to 18–34 until both drive the same distance per B-permit holder, as if the whole gap were young drivers' driving registered to owners aged 35 to 54: 15.3 billion km, 13% of the 35–54 kilometres. It is arithmetic on the published table, not an estimate: the data say neither how much of the gap is young drivers' driving nor which older band holds it. Under it, involvement per km at 18–34 falls from 2.57× to 1.55× the 35–54 rate and deaths per km from 2.43× to 1.47×; at 75 and over, involvement per km goes from 1.02× to 0.89× and deaths per km from 3.99× to 3.48×. The second gap, if it is cars driven by others, pulls the other way: kilometres credited to owners of 75 and over but driven by younger people make that band's published per-km rates too low, by an amount the data do not give. Cars registered to companies, 14% of car kilometres, have no owner age: their kilometres leave the denominator while their drivers stay in the numerator. Spreading them over every band in proportion changes no ratio; a scenario that spreads them over the bands from 18 to 64 raises the 75-and-over deaths-per-km ratio from 3.99× to 4.76×. None of this moves deaths per driver involved.

No band can serve as a reference where owner and driver are known to coincide. Cars per B-permit holder are 0.93 at 55–64 and 0.94 at 65–74, but owning a car per licence does not show that the owner drives it, so the page keeps 35–54 as the reference and gives each per-km ratio as a range. Against that reference, involvement per owner-age km is lower at 55–64 and 65–74 under both readings: 0.63–0.72× at 55–64 and 0.62–0.71× at 65–74. Against 65–74, whose kilometres the transfer scenario does not move, drivers aged 75 and over were involved 1.43× (1.38–1.49) as often per owner-age km and killed 2.71× (1.92–3.83) as often, with 1.90× (1.34–2.68) the deaths per driver involved.

Residents, B-permit holders, drivers involved, kilometres

The same driver deaths give a different ratio under each denominator. Against drivers aged 35 to 54, car drivers aged 75 and over died 1.23 times as often per resident, 3.10 times per B-permit holder, 3.93 times per driver involved and 3.99 times per km of cars registered to owners of the same age (the published end of the range above). Residents and permit holders count people, not driving. In 2024, 38% of residents aged 75 and over held a licence of any class against 83% of those aged 45–54, and from 35–44 on the gap between the sexes widens with age: 74% of men over 74 held one and 13% of women. Of licence holders aged 75 and over, 83% held a B permit. The licence counts include moped and motorcycle permits and the agricultural licence. A per-resident rate for older women divides by a population most of whom hold no licence at all.

Men and women: per licence holder and per driver involved

No file in this repository measures kilometres driven by sex, so men and women are compared per licence holder and per driver involved. In 2022–2024, male car drivers were involved in injury crashes 1.41× (1.40–1.42) as often as female ones per licence holder; once involved they were killed 2.57× (2.24–2.94) as often; together, 3.63× (3.17–4.15) the deaths per licence holder. Involvement per licence holder mixes how much each sex drives with how often they crash for the distance driven, and the data cannot separate the two. Deaths per driver involved start from drivers already in a crash, so they need no measure of driving; they say how often a recorded crash ended in the driver's death, not why.

Conclusion

Recorded in DGT's 2024 car-driver tables: the share of drivers in an injury crash who were killed rose with age after 35–54, to 3.93× at 75 and over, a ratio that needs no exposure. Per kilometre the repository holds only kilometres driven by cars registered to owners of the same age; on that denominator drivers aged 75 and over were killed 3.48–3.99× as often as drivers aged 35 to 54, and drivers aged 18 to 34 were involved in injury crashes 1.55–2.57× as often, with a share killed once involved not distinguishable from 35–54 (0.95×). In 2022–2024 male car drivers were killed 3.63× as often per licence holder as female ones, with 1.41× the involvement and 2.57× the deaths per driver involved. These are associations in recorded counts; the tables do not say what produces them.

Limits. The per-km denominator is the kilometres driven by cars registered to owners of the driver's age band: owner age stands in for driver age, company cars have no owner age, and the transfer and company-car scenarios are arithmetic on the published table, not estimates of who drives. The kilometres are modelled from roadworthiness-inspection odometer readings and are valid for aggregates only; the intervals come from the crash counts and treat them as known. The numerator counts drivers of cars on Spanish roads, including foreign-registered cars and unlicensed drivers, while the kilometres cover Spanish-registered cars only. Drivers whose age or sex the tables do not record are left out. The kilometre comparison is one year, 2024. Deaths per driver involved count the driver only. No file in the repository measures kilometres by sex, so the sex comparison is per licence holder of any class and per driver involved.
