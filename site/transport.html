Two questions are kept apart here. Representativeness: how do Catalonia's and Barcelona's crashes differ from Spain's? That needs no model, only the DGT records, which use one definition everywhere. Transportability: does a model trained in one place keep its ranking on records from another? Every transferred score is shown beside a model trained inside the target domain. The Catalan model ranks Barcelona's crashes at 0.642, against a native 0.656 for a model trained in Barcelona itself (gap -0.015). The fall of 0.12 from what it achieves inside the rest of Catalonia splits into 0.05 for Barcelona's smaller training set, 0.05 because Barcelona's crashes are harder to rank with these variables (other urban Catalan crashes at the same training size are harder still: -0.04), and 0.01 for the move itself, an interval that includes zero. Restricted to the variables DGT records the same way, the model ranks crashes elsewhere in Spain at 0.708, against a native 0.712 (gap -0.004). Transfer shows that associations hold elsewhere; it does not make them causes.

A positive gap means the transferred model, trained on many more crashes, beat a model trained on the small target domain: the training size outweighed the change of place. Trained on Barcelona alone and tested on the rest of Catalonia the model scores 0.58: Barcelona is not a proxy for Catalonia.

Why Barcelona is harder

The fall from the rest of Catalonia to Barcelona is split into parts that each have their own measurement: the cost of Barcelona's smaller training set, how much harder Barcelona's crashes are to rank with the same features and training size, and what is left, the true cost of moving the model. They add up to the total.

C. The outward path toward Spain

Five stages, in order: held-out rows of the same source; later years; another region inside the source; another independently recorded Spanish dataset; and national aggregates showing whether the training population resembles Spain. A model is called potentially nationally transferable only if it passes all five. No model jumps from Barcelona or Catalonia to Spain without the stages between.

Conclusion

Inside Catalonia the model keeps its ranking in a later year and in each demarcation left out of training. Barcelona's crashes are harder to rank than the rest of Catalonia's, as other urban crashes are, and the move itself costs no measurable ranking. On the variables recorded alike, the Catalan model keeps its ranking on crashes recorded elsewhere in Spain. No model passes every stage of the outward path, so none is called nationally transferable. None of this is causal.

Limits. Transfer is predictive evidence only; how often fields are left unrecorded differs between regions, and the data do not establish whether that is recording practice or the crashes; no person-level or full-feature national validation is possible with the data in the repository.
