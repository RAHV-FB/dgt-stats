Over time

Long-run trends in road deaths, 1993–2024

Road deaths in Spain fell 10.9% a year from 2003 to 2013 and have shown no clear rise or fall since: 1,680 people died in 2013 and 1,785 in 2024. Deaths per tonne of road fuel sold, which stands in for traffic, were 16% and 14% above their pre-pandemic trend in 2023 and 2024, an excess that depends on where the trend starts and on the kilometres a tonne of fuel represents; per measured interurban kilometre, deaths stayed within their trend's range.

Deaths fell steeply from 2003 to 2013 and have levelled off since

Between 1993 and 2013 the annual count of deaths fell 74%, from 6,378 to 1,680, most of it after 2003. Since 2013 the count has no clear trend: fitted alone, the count of 2013–2019 rises 1.2% a year (95% interval −0.2% to +2.7%).

In 2023 and 2024 deaths per tonne of fuel were above their pre-pandemic trend, while the count was within its range

Each trend fitted up to 2019 is projected forward with a 95% prediction interval, its range: where a year would be expected to fall had the trend's last segment gone on with its ordinary scatter. The count was below its range in 2020 and 2021 and has been within it since 2022. Deaths per tonne of fuel stayed within range in those years, because fuel sales fell too, and were 16% and 14% above the trend in 2023 and 2024, outside its range.

Started in 2013, where the count turned, instead of 2011, the trend leaves smaller excesses: 10% and 8%, at the edge of the range in 2023 and within the range in 2024. Had the kilometres a tonne of fuel represents grown 1.3 percentage points a year faster from 2020 than before, the 2023 excess would also fall within the range; for 2024, 0.6 points would be enough. The projections under other choices are in the technical notes.

On interurban roads, deaths per measured kilometre stayed within their trend's range

The Ministerio de Transportes measures the vehicle-kilometres driven on State, regional and provincial interurban roads. Per measured kilometre, interurban deaths in 2023 were 5% above the trend, inside its range of ±12%. Roads run by municipalities and other bodies, where 8.7% to 11.2% of interurban deaths occurred each year from 2016 to 2024, are not measured; that moves the comparison by less than 1%.

Road fuel stands in for kilometres driven, which no source counts on all roads

The kilometres a tonne of fuel represents can change with fuel economy, electric driving, the mix of freight and private travel, and fuel bought in Spain but burnt elsewhere, so a change per tonne cannot be read as a change per kilometre. Divided by national road fuel instead, as a check on road fuel as a measure of traffic, the interurban deaths were 13% above their trend in 2023, outside its range.

The measures part because measured kilometres per tonne of fuel grew 1.4 percentage points a year faster after 2019 than before; had kilometres per tonne grown that much faster on all roads, the 2023 excess in deaths per tonne of fuel would fall within its range. Whether they did is unknown, so the excess of deaths per tonne of fuel stays unresolved.

Against 2019, deaths in 2024 changed within ordinary variation under every denominator

Spain recorded 1,785 road deaths in 2024, 1.7% more than in 2019. Per resident they fell 1.9% and per tonne of road fuel sold they rose 3.5%, because the population grew while fuel sales fell. People admitted to hospital after a crash rose 13.0% per tonne of road fuel, beyond ordinary variation, an excess that would vanish had kilometres per tonne of fuel grown 0.3% a year faster.

An ordinary year's variation is how far each annual count moved around its trend over 2013–2019, the last segment of the count's trend before the pandemic: the variance of deaths was 1.5 times what pure chance gives, and that of admissions 7.7 times. The intervals allow for that scatter, with Student's t for the trend fit's five residual degrees of freedom, and treat every denominator as exact.

Deaths per tonne of fuel fell by three quarters, but the records cannot tell fewer crashes from less deadly ones

Deaths per tonne of road fuel fell 76% between 1996 and 2024. Split by injury crashes, crashes per tonne fell 13% and deaths per crash 73%; split by people admitted to hospital, admissions per tonne fell 79% and deaths per admission rose 15%. Each split depends on how completely the lesser casualties are recorded, so how the fall divides between how often crashes happen and how deadly they are cannot be told from these series.

Conventional roads have 3 to 4 times the deaths per kilometre of motorways

On the networks whose traffic is measured, conventional roads, single and dual carriageway, had 7.41 deaths per billion vehicle-kilometres in 2023 and motorways (autopistas and autovías) 2.15: 3.45 times as many (95% interval 3.03–3.92). Over 2016–2023 the ratio ran from 3.04 to 3.84.

Limitations. DGT estimated the 30-day deaths of 1993 to 2010 from deaths within 24 hours, with correction factors; since 2011 it has counted them. Refitted on deaths within 24 hours, which the police count directly throughout, the trends turn in the same years.
