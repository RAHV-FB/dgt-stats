1993 to 2024: structural change and the pandemic

Spain's road deaths fell slowly until 2003, steeply for 10 years, and have not fallen since 2013. As a count, 2020 was −24% against the pre-pandemic trend, below its interval. Divided by road fuel sold, 2020 and 2021 were −3% and −3% against the pre-2020 per-fuel trend, inside its interval: deaths fell in line with the fuel sold. By 2023 and 2024, deaths per tonne of road fuel were +17% and +16% above where that trend was heading, outside its interval. Road fuel is a proxy for kilometres that no series here calibrates on all roads, so that is an excess per tonne of fuel sold, not per kilometre. On interurban roads, where the Ministerio de Transportes measures the kilometres driven, deaths per measured kilometre in 2023 were +5% on their own pre-2020 trend, inside its interval, which runs from −4% to +15%.

The three measures find the same history. The turning points are placed by the data (every combination of up to three is fitted and the simplest adequate model kept), and they land within 2 years of each other: a slow decline to 2002–2003, a fall of between 10.7% and 13.4% a year for 9–10 years, then a halt. In the last segment the count changes +0.7% a year and occupant deaths per registered vehicle +0.1%, both with intervals that include no change; deaths per tonne of road fuel still fall, −2.2% a year. The per-vehicle measure counts only the occupants of motorcycles, cars, vans, trucks and buses, the people a registered vehicle can carry.

As a count, 2020 was −24% against trend and 2024 −4%. Occupant deaths per registered vehicle were −26% in 2020 and −17% in 2021, below the interval: the fleet, which counts vehicles whether used or not, grew in 2020 while deaths fell. From 2022 the count and the per-vehicle measure are both inside their intervals. Per tonne of fuel, 2020 was −3%, inside the interval, and 2024 +16%, outside it.

The per-fuel projection assumes that whatever moved kilometres per tonne over 2011–2019 went on at the same pace after it. No series in the repository measures kilometres per tonne on all roads, so that assumption cannot be checked. As hypotheticals only: with kilometres per tonne growing an extra 1% a year from 2020 the ratio would be +13% in 2023 (outside the interval) and +10% in 2024 (inside the interval); with an extra 2% a year, +8% in 2023 (inside the interval) and +5% in 2024 (inside the interval). The excess would fall inside the interval with an extra 1.4 points a year for 2023 and 0.7 for 2024. These are conditions, not estimates, and the page draws no conclusion from them.

Interurban roads: deaths per measured kilometre

The Ministerio de Transportes publishes the vehicle-kilometres travelled each year on the interurban roads of the State, the regions and the provincial councils, measured by their traffic counts. Divided into the yearbook's interurban deaths, that is the one series here whose numerator and denominator cover nearly the same roads. Nearly: the deaths also include interurban roads run by municipalities and other bodies, which the kilometres leave out. The DGT microdata, which reproduce the yearbook's interurban count in every year from 2016 to 2024, record who runs the road: between 8.7% and 11.2% of interurban deaths each year were on roads outside the counted networks, 10.1% in 2019 and 9.9% in 2023. The level of the rate is too high by about that share; the 2019 to 2023 comparison moves by under one point. Before 2016 the share cannot be measured.

Fitting the same turning-point search to interurban deaths per measured kilometre, 2008–2019, places the last turning point in 2013 and finds a change of −1.8% a year after it. Projected on with each year's kilometres, 2023 sits +5% on that trend, inside the interval, and 2020 at 1.00× the trend.

The same interurban deaths divided by national road fuel give a different projection: 2023 at +13% on that trend, outside its interval. That is not a second rate: fuel sold for every road, towns included, is not the exposure of interurban deaths, and it is shown only as a diagnostic of the fuel proxy. The two exposures part after 2019: measured interurban kilometres per tonne of national road fuel were 8,741 in 2019 and 9,420 in 2023. That ratio mixes scopes, so it is not a measure of fuel economy, and nothing here uses it to correct the all-road per-fuel series.

Full results: observed against trend, every year, segments and annual changes, the turning-point search, the per-fuel ratio under hypothetical drifts, interurban deaths, kilometres and fuel, the trend re-run on kilometres, with the fuel diagnostic, interurban deaths by who runs the road (CSV).

Conclusion

Spanish road deaths fell most between about 2002–2003 and 2013, and as a count they have not fallen since. In 2020 the count fell below its pre-pandemic trend while deaths per tonne of road fuel stayed inside the interval of theirs. From 2022 the count and occupant deaths per registered vehicle are inside their intervals; deaths per tonne of road fuel are above theirs in 2023 and 2024, but fuel is a proxy whose relation to kilometres is not measured on all roads. On interurban roads, the one network where kilometres are measured, deaths per kilometre in 2023 were inside the interval of their pre-2020 trend. These series show no rise in deaths per kilometre on interurban roads up to 2023; they cannot say what happened per kilometre on all roads.

Limits. The trends are statistical descriptions, not explanations, and a projection assumes the last segment would have continued. Road fuel is fuel sold, a proxy for traffic whose relation to kilometres is not measured on all roads. The measured kilometres cover the interurban roads of the State, the regions and the provincial councils, run to 2023 and change basis in 2008; between 8.7% and 11.2% of interurban deaths in 2016–2024 were on other roads. The registered fleet counts vehicles whether used or not, and the per-vehicle measure counts only occupants of motorcycles, cars, vans, trucks and buses. The 30-day death series is taken as DGT publishes it for every year since 1993; the repository does not document whether its definition changed over that period.
