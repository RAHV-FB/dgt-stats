What an interrupted series can and cannot show

An interrupted time series fits the months before a change, projects that pattern forward, and asks whether the months after sit below it. It needs a stable pre-trend, a post-period free of other changes, and a way to tell a real break from the ordinary wobble of the series. The last point is handled here with placebos: the same model is refitted with the intervention placed at every other admissible month, and a real effect should sit in the tail of that distribution. The wording follows the result: coincided with unless the pre-trend, the placebos and the sensitivity fits all agree.

The points-based licence, 1 July 2006

Deaths were already falling before July 2006: the fitted trend over January 2000 to June 2006 is -4.7% a year. Against that trend and the seasonal pattern, the level of the series shifts by -12.0% at July 2006 (-17.2% to -6.4%), and the slope afterwards is +4.2% a year relative to the pre-trend (-2.5% to +11.4%), not distinguishable from no change. Over the 17 months to November 2007 the fitted counterfactual exceeds the observed deaths by 586. The raw comparison points the same way: the twelve months from July 2006 had 3,862 deaths against 4,361 in the twelve months before (-11.4%), of which the pre-trend alone explains about -4.7%.

With the break placed at any of the 38 other months from January 2002 to February 2005, the estimated level change runs from -8.1% to +8.4%; the July 2006 estimate ranks 1 of 39. The placebos are not noise around zero: 24 of the 38 are negative, and all 12 breaks placed in 2004 come out negative because the decline steepened that year, which is the pre-trend problem in another form. The July 2006 drop is larger than any of them.

The estimate survives the 24-hour definition, the interurban series, the fleet offset and a negative-binomial fit. It does not survive two variants. On urban streets alone the change is -7.6% with an interval that includes zero: the drop is an interurban one. Extending the window to December 2009 with a second break at the Penal Code reform, the July 2006 level change falls to -6.0% (-12.9% to +1.5%) and the December 2007 break takes -7.9%: the two changes share the decline, and the recession of 2008 is not in the model at all. Starting the pre-period in 1993 gives -20.4%, but that fit is misspecified (the series is flat to 2003 and then falls, so one line does not describe it) and its dispersion of 3.2 says so.

Reading. July 2006 coincided with a drop of about 12% in monthly road deaths that no other break month from January 2002 to February 2005 reproduces and that holds under most alternative specifications. Whether the licence caused it cannot be settled on this series: the speed-camera programme arrived in the same two years, the Penal Code reform 17 months later, and the recession after that. What the series supports is that the decline from mid-2006 was a step, not the continuation of the pre-trend.

The 90 km/h limit on conventional roads, 29 January 2019

Here the series cannot isolate conventional roads, but the microdata can. The treated group is DGT's two conventional-road codes (single and double carriageway), the control group its motorway and dual-carriageway codes (autopista de peaje, autopista libre and autovía), whose limits did not change; the control stands in for weather, traffic and everything else the two share. The contrast is diluted on the treated side: some single-carriageway roads were already at 90 km/h before the decree, which the microdata cannot separate from the rest; the double-carriageway conventional roads it also covered are a fifth to a quarter of the treated deaths in the clean window (22% of them, from 20% in 2016 to 25% in 2019); and about 5% of the treated deaths are on the urban crossings (travesías) those roads run through, where the urban limit applied and the decree changed nothing. In one model of both groups, conventional roads show a change of -13.1% (-18.6% to -7.3%) at February 2019 over and above the control roads, whose own change is +4.9%.

The design fails its own check. A break placed in January 2017 gives +12.3% (+3.1% to +22.4%) relative to the control, with an interval that excludes zero: the two groups of roads did not move together in the years before the limit, so a divergence at February 2019 cannot be told from the ordinary divergence of the two series. The other placebo (January 2018, +1.0%) is as it should be.

Reading. The clean-window estimate for the 90 km/h limit, -13%, is not distinguishable from the swings the two series show against each other in the years before the limit: a placebo break in January 2017 gives a divergence of its own. No claim about the limit's effect can be made from these data; a road-section series with speeds and traffic volumes would be needed.

Limits

Monthly deaths are counts with overdispersion (the Pearson dispersion of the main 2006 fit is 2.1) and serial correlation; the standard errors are Newey–West with 12 lags (within each road group in the 2019 panel) and the negative-binomial variant is in each table. The 2006 model has no exposure series but the annual fleet, interpolated to months. The 2019 model treats the road type recorded at the crash as fixed, but DGT's road-type coding moves three times in the period: in 2021 most crashes on double-carriageway conventional roads are recoded as single-carriageway conventional, which is why the two codes form one group here; in 2022 and again in 2024 the toll and free motorway codes swap, which does not affect the contrast because both are in the control group; and in 2024 a large share of urban streets is recoded as other road types (see the data page). The clean window ends in February 2020 and is not affected by any of them, and the extended fit is not affected by the 2024 recoding either: the streets it moves go to a road-type code that is in neither group, and across that break the treated deaths go from 876 to 873 and the control deaths from 346 to 362. Neither design can attribute a change to one measure when several arrived together, which is why the page says coincided.
