Over time

The 2006 points-based licence

Spain's points-based driving licence came into force on 1 July 2006. Road deaths fell around that date, but the series cannot show that the licence caused the fall, or that it lasted: averaged over the 17 months to November 2007, deaths were 2.5% below the projection from the earlier trend (95% interval −7.8% to +3.2%). The model's intervals are too narrow: at 6 of the 14 other Julys, when nothing was introduced, it finds a step whose interval excludes zero.

An immediate fall of about 7% that did not last

A Poisson regression of monthly deaths within 30 days from January 2000 to November 2007, with a term for each calendar month, a trend, and a step and a change of slope at July 2006, finds an immediate step of −7% (95% interval −13% to −1%). The slope then changes by +7.5% a year, so fitted deaths rejoin the projected path in about July 2007 and end the window 2.3% above it; averaged over the 17 months, the change is −2.5%.

The result depends on how the earlier trend is projected. A straight line gives a step of −12% and a lasting fall, −9.5% on average (−14.3% to −4.5%), but fits the months before the change less well than a trend that bends once, in August 2003, where the decline steepened: by 7 points of QAIC, a measure of fit that penalises extra terms. That difference favours the bend without being decisive.

Falls nearly as large occur in Julys when nothing changed

Deaths usually peak in July or August, so a break placed on 1 July can pick up an ordinary summer movement. Fitted with a straight-line trend at July 2006 and at every other July from 1998 to 2018 whose window avoids it and the pandemic, the same kind of model puts the July 2006 step at −9.8%, the largest of 15, but only narrowly: July 2004 gives −7.2% and July 2001 −5.5%, with overlapping intervals.

The same fits show the model's intervals to be too narrow: at 6 of the 14 other Julys the 95% interval excludes zero, where about 0.7 would be expected. An interval set by the spread of the placebo steps puts July 2006 at −21% to +3%, which includes no change. Forecasts made before each July agree: the months after July 2006 fall 7.1% below their forecast, the third largest of 15.

The fall cannot be attributed to the licence

Adding national road-fuel consumption (CORES) or traffic intensity on the state toll motorways barely moves the step, to −8% in both cases, against −7% without either, but neither series measures kilometres on all roads. Within the fitted window the step competes with two other explanations: the decline had already steepened in 2003, and Julys when nothing was introduced show steps nearly as large.

The window ends in November 2007, before the Penal Code reform that made serious speeding and drink-driving criminal offences and before the recession that followed; over a longer window they cannot be separated from the licence. Every specification tried, and the twelve-month comparisons, are in the technical notes.
