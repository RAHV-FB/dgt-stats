Spain

Recorded crash factors

How often the police recorded alcohol, inappropriate speed, distraction, illegal manoeuvres and drugs in injury crashes from 2014 to 2023, and which of those series can be compared from year to year.

DGT's speed report counts, for each year from 2014 to 2023, the injury crashes in Spain outside Catalonia and the Basque Country in which the police recorded each of five factors: alcohol, inappropriate speed, distraction, illegal manoeuvres and drugs. Where the recording is consistent from year to year, recorded alcohol rose on interurban roads from 5.5% of injury crashes in 2014 to 8.2% in 2023, and recorded inappropriate speed fell across all roads from 10.0% to 6.9%. Distraction on urban streets and drug-related crashes cannot be followed across the period: their series jump in ways that point to changes in recording.

Each share is the proportion of injury crashes in which officers recorded the factor. A crash can carry several factors, so the shares overlap, and a recorded factor is a police judgement about the crash, not a finding that it caused the crash. The shares describe the police record of crashes that happened; they do not measure how often drivers drink, speed or are distracted on the road. The report gives deaths only for crashes with speed recorded, which Speed compares with other crashes; for the other factors deaths per crash cannot be computed.

Which years can be compared

A factor's share can move because something changed on the road, because the way crashes are recorded changed, or both. Every year-to-year change in every series was tested against a fixed rule: a rise of more than 25% or a fall of more than 20% in a single year, or fewer than 200 crashes in either year, marks a break in comparability, and a series is compared only within the runs of years between its breaks. Of the 135 year-to-year changes, 33 are breaks or too small to test. The rule finds discontinuities; it cannot say whether a break, or a trend within a run, comes from behaviour, from recording or from both. The threshold is a convention, and every change is published with its test so that another threshold can be applied.

Recorded alcohol rose on interurban roads across the whole period without a break, from 5.5% to 8.2% of injury crashes. On urban streets the series breaks in 2016 and rises within the later run, from 2.5% in 2016 to 3.2% in 2023. More recorded alcohol can reflect more drinking drivers, more breath tests after crashes, or both, and these data cannot separate them.

Recorded inappropriate speed fell on both kinds of road without a break: from 18.6% to 14.3% of injury crashes on interurban roads and from 4.1% to 3.1% on urban streets. Distraction or inattention on interurban roads has no break and stayed between 24.8% and 28.8% of crashes. Illegal manoeuvres, a composite of priority, distance, overtaking and negligent driving, rose on interurban roads from 25.4% to 37.8%, a level they had already reached by 2017.

Series that break

Recorded distraction on urban streets rose 68% in 2016 and fell 58% in 2019, while the interurban series moved smoothly, so the urban series is compared only within 2016–2018 and 2019–2023. Recorded alcohol on urban streets rose 57% in the same year, 2016, which is also the year DGT's driver tables lost the speed status of half of drivers (from 19% to 52% unrecorded); the reason for the shared timing is unknown. Drugs were recorded in at most 838 crashes a year, and the count climbed 12-fold from 2014 to 2018 before collapsing in 2020. Every year-to-year change in the drug series breaks the rule or has too few crashes, so it is not interpreted.
