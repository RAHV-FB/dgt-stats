Spain

Recorded crash factors

DGT's thematic report on speed counts, for each year from 2014 to 2023, the injury crashes in Spain outside Catalonia and the Basque Country in which the police recorded alcohol, inappropriate speed, distraction, illegal manoeuvres or drugs.

Within years of consistent recording, the share of injury crashes in which the police recorded alcohol rose on interurban roads from 5.5% in 2014 to 8.2% in 2023, and the share with inappropriate speed recorded fell from 10.0% to 6.9% across all roads. The series for distraction on urban streets breaks twice and is compared only within the runs between its breaks; the drug series breaks, or is too small to test, between every pair of consecutive years.

Each share is the proportion of injury crashes in which officers recorded the factor; a crash can carry several factors, so the shares overlap. The shares describe the police record of crashes that happened and do not measure how often drivers drink, speed or are distracted on the road (Methodology).

Comparable years

A factor's share can move because something changed on the road or because the way crashes are recorded changed. Every year-to-year change in every factor's share was therefore tested against a fixed rule: a rise of more than 25% or a fall of more than 20% in a single year (the same ratio in either direction), or fewer than 200 crashes in either year, marks a break in comparability. A series is compared only within the runs of years between its breaks. Of the 135 year-to-year changes, 33 are breaks or too small to test. The rule locates a discontinuity; whether a break, or a trend within a run, is behavioural or recording-related is left open. The threshold is a convention, and every change is published with its test result so that another threshold can be applied.

Recorded alcohol rose on interurban roads across the whole period without a break, from 5.5% to 8.2% of injury crashes. On urban streets the series breaks in 2016 and rises within the later run, from 2.5% in 2016 to 3.2% in 2023. More recorded alcohol can mean more drinking drivers or more breath tests after crashes; DGT's enforcement statistics, which would separate the two, are outside this study.

Recorded inappropriate speed fell on both kinds of road without a break: from 18.6% to 14.3% of injury crashes on interurban roads and from 4.1% to 3.1% on urban streets. Distraction or inattention on interurban roads has no break and stayed between 24.8% and 28.8% of crashes. Illegal manoeuvres, a composite of priority, distance, overtaking and negligent driving, rose on interurban roads from 25.4% to 37.8%, a level they had already reached by 2017.

Series with breaks in recording

Recorded distraction on urban streets rose 68% in 2016 and fell 58% in 2019, while the interurban series has no break. The urban series is therefore compared only within 2016–2018 and 2019–2023. Recorded alcohol on urban streets rose 57% in the same year, 2016. Drugs were recorded in at most 838 crashes a year; the count climbed 12-fold from 2014 to 2018 and collapsed in 2020. Every year-to-year change in the drug series either has too few crashes or breaks the rule, so the drug series is left uninterpreted. In 2016, too, the share of drivers with no speed status in DGT's driver tables jumped to 52%, from 19% the year before; the reason for the shared timing is unknown.

Limitations. The report gives deaths only for crashes with inappropriate speed recorded, which Speed compares with other crashes. Deaths per crash with alcohol, distraction or the other factors recorded cannot be computed from it.
