Crash severity

Crash severity model

Among crashes in Catalonia in which someone was killed or seriously injured, the Catalan severity model estimates the share that were fatal (someone died within 24 hours) from the road, the conditions, the type of crash and who was involved. Predicting each year from 2016 to 2023 from earlier years only, it matched the overall fatal share (though not in 2016) and sorted crashes better than a simple table, but missed in some provinces.

The estimates matched later years overall, but not in every province

Each year was predicted by a model whose settings and coefficients came only from the years before it. Over the 11,611 crashes of 2016–2023 the model estimated 12.4% fatal, and 12.3% were (11.7–12.9%).

Of the fifth of crashes it rated most likely to have been fatal, 30% were; of the fifth it rated least likely, 3%. A table of fatal shares by road and crash type separates them less (27% and 4%): the gain is real but modest. The model cannot say whether a crash will happen, only how often crashes like a given one were fatal once recorded with a death or serious injury.

By province and kind of road the estimates were less close. On urban streets in Barcelona, interurban roads in Girona and interurban roads in Tarragona the mean estimate fell outside the 95% interval of the observed share (the figures), so an estimate for those roads should not be taken at face value.

Crashes involving a heavy vehicle: about twice the fatal share

Starting from a typical crash on a regional road and changing one thing at a time shows which circumstances the records associate with a fatal outcome. With a lorry or bus involved, the estimated fatal share is 1.98 times that of the same crash between cars (95% confidence interval 1.75–2.24), the largest increase of any input. A crash within a junction carries 0.76 times the share of one between junctions. Refitted separately on 2010–2016 and on 2017–2023, seven of the eleven changes below kept the same direction; the other four should not be read as settled.

Tested only within Catalonia

No other source records the model's inputs, so it has been tested only within Catalonia. With each province left out of its fitting, it still ranked that province's crashes better than the table, but its estimates of the fatal share missed for Barcelona, Girona and Tarragona; fitted without Barcelona city, it put the city's fatal share too low. It should not be used for a place it was not fitted on. The project's Barcelona models are not used as predictors: one ranked crashes no better than a table, and the other can only rank people.

Try the model in the crash severity calculator. How it was built and every test are on Model method and tests, and every model the project fitted is re-evaluated in the model review.

Limitations. The model describes crashes recorded in Catalonia in 2010–2023 on roads with a named owning network or of another type. “Fatal” means a death within 24 hours, the Catalan definition; Spain's official figures count deaths within 30 days.
