Severity models: which ones earn their place

Models trained on individual crash and person records, each compared with a plain table of outcome shares and judged on later records it never saw. Only the ones that beat the table are kept.

Layers: Crash microdata: Catalonia; Rich microdata: Barcelona. Units: one crash with a death or serious injury; one crash; one person record.

Every model here is asked one question before anything else: does it rank recorded crashes or people better than a plain table of outcome shares? The Catalonia model and the Barcelona person model do, on later records they never saw. The Barcelona crash model does not: its table is shown instead. None of them is causal, none predicts whether a crash happens, and none measures risk per trip or kilometre: the data have no exposure.

Catalonia: which serious crashes were fatal

Among recorded crashes with a death or serious injury, which road, crash and environmental characteristics go with a fatal outcome, beyond the crash type and zone?

Train 2010-2020, choose on 2021-2022, test 2023. Selected on validation data: boosted trees, ROC-AUC 0.79 (0.76–0.82) and PR-AUC 0.41 against a prevalence of 0.12. Calibration slope 0.97 and mean prediction 11.9% against 12.1% observed: its probabilities can be read as estimates for groups of similar cases.

Against a table of outcome shares by D SUBTIPUS ACCIDENT x D SUBZONA (40 groups, built on the training rows), on the same test rows: ROC-AUC 0.79 for the model, 0.70 for the table, a gain of +0.09 (+0.07 to +0.12). The model adds signal over the table.

The second version adds the police's influence judgements and the fields whose completeness follows the outcome (a retrospective administrative model): ROC-AUC 0.80 on the same test rows. It is kept as a diagnostic of the record, never as a predictor.

Finer geography: with the most granular place variable the training fit is 0.89 while the test score is 0.78, against 0.79 with the broad one.

Barcelona: which people were seriously hurt

Among people recorded in Barcelona crashes, which circumstances and road-user characteristics go with a serious or fatal injury, beyond road role and vehicle?

Train months 1-9 with 5-fold cross-validation grouped by crash; test months 10-12. Selected on validation data: boosted trees, ROC-AUC 0.84 (0.81–0.87) and PR-AUC 0.07 against a prevalence of 0.01. Calibration slope 0.63 and mean prediction 1.4% against 1.4% observed: the probabilities are not reliable as the share of similar cases with the outcome, so the model is used only to rank profiles.

Against a table of outcome shares by person role x associated vehicle group (35 groups, built on the training rows), on the same test rows: ROC-AUC 0.84 for the model, 0.78 for the table, a gain of +0.06 (+0.02 to +0.11). The model adds signal over the table.

The second version adds the police's recorded causes (a retrospective classification, not a real-time prediction): ROC-AUC 0.85 on the same test rows. It is kept as a diagnostic of the record, never as a predictor.

Finer geography: with the most granular place variable the training fit is 1.00 while the test score is 0.81, against 0.84 with the broad one.

Where the person model works, and where it does not

Every person is scored by a model that never saw their crash (grouped cross-validation over the year). A ROC-AUC is shown only where a group has at least 20 serious or fatal cases (13 groups). Spain: a national test, not a national model

No model on this page is trained on the national DGT crash records: their audit fails 2 of its checks (comparable across regions, recording artefacts do not dominate; sources page), so they describe Spain rather than train a model of it. They are used instead to test the Catalan model, restricted to the variables both sources record the same way, on DGT's crash records from outside Catalonia, a separately published dataset: the target domain's own model scores 0.712, the transferred Catalan model 0.708, a gap of -0.004 on 67,971 crashes (10,457 fatal), calibration slope 1.10. How far that reaches is on the generalisability page. The DGT records also support an association analysis, a supporting analysis and not a predictive model. The monthly deaths series has a forecast that does not beat repeating last year's count in ordinary years, so it is not featured as a model either.

Models that were not built

Predicting whether a cause such as alcohol, drugs or speed was recorded is not a defensible task here. 9 recorded causes have fewer than 100 crashes (insufficient data for a reliable model, and no synthetic oversampling is used to pretend otherwise); the 13 with more are compared descriptively on the Barcelona page instead: a recorded cause is the police's coding after the event, and the files do not show how fully each crash was investigated or tested.

Conclusion

2 of the 3 models trained on regional records rank severity better than a table of outcome shares and hold on later records; 1 does not, and its table replaces it. The rankings are associations for prioritising analysis, not causes and not risks.

Limits. Catalonia: serious and fatal crashes only. Barcelona: one year, few serious cases, intervals in the tables. No exposure anywhere, so no risk. How far the models travel is on the next page; full leakage audit in ML_LEAKAGE_AUDIT.md.
