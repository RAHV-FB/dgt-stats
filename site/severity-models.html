Crash severity

Crash severity model

Among crashes in Catalonia in which someone was killed or seriously injured, a statistical model estimates the share that were fatal (someone died within 24 hours) from the road, the conditions, the type of crash and who was involved. Predicting each year from 2016 to 2023 from earlier years only, it matched the overall fatal share and ranked crashes better than a simple table, but only moderately (ROC-AUC 0.74), and not at all on roads through towns. A heavy vehicle involved goes with about twice the fatal share.

Crashes involving a heavy vehicle: about twice the fatal share

Starting from a typical crash on a regional road and changing one thing at a time shows which circumstances the records associate with a fatal outcome. With a lorry or bus involved, the estimated fatal share is 1.98 times that of the same crash between cars (95% confidence interval 1.75–2.24), the largest increase of any input. A crash within a junction carries 0.76 times the share of one between junctions. Refitted separately on 2010–2016 and on 2017–2023, six of the eight changes below kept the same direction; the other two should not be read as settled.

It ranks crashes better than a table, but only moderately

Each year was predicted by a model whose settings and coefficients came only from the years before it. A table of the fatal share of each road and crash type, fitted on the same years, is the benchmark. Over the 11,611 crashes of 2016–2023 the model's ROC-AUC, the chance that it rates a fatal crash above a non-fatal one, is 0.74 and the table's 0.71; the model's lead is 0.02–0.04 (95% interval), and it is ahead in every year from 2016 to 2023. On roads through towns it does not beat the table (0.55 against 0.58). Within one kind of road the ranking is harder, because the kind of road alone separates many fatal crashes from the rest: the model ranks interurban crashes best (0.70) and urban ones less well (0.66).

Of the fifth of crashes it rated most likely to have been fatal, 30% were; of the fifth it rated least likely, 3%. The table separates them less (27% and 4%): the gain is real but modest. The model cannot say whether a crash will happen, only how often crashes like a given one were fatal once recorded with a death or serious injury.

The estimates matched later years overall, but not in every province

Over all the years the model estimated 12.4% fatal, and 12.3% were (11.7–12.9%); its calibration slope is 1.03, where 1 means the estimates spread as widely as the outcomes. The mean estimate fell outside the observed interval in 2016. By province and kind of road the estimates were less close. On on urban streets in the province of Barcelona, 7.54% estimated and 6.70% observed (6.02–7.45%), on interurban roads in the province of Girona, 23.2% estimated and 26.8% observed (23.8–30.1%) and on interurban roads in the province of Tarragona, 26.7% estimated and 31.0% observed (27.8–34.4%) the mean estimate fell outside the 95% interval of the observed share, so an estimate for those roads should not be taken at face value. On urban streets outside Barcelona city the estimates ran high, 7.1% estimated and 6.0% observed (5.4–6.7%).

Tested only within Catalonia

No other source records the model's inputs, so it has been tested only on crashes from the same file: the last year, fitted on the years before it, each province left out of the fitting in turn, and Barcelona city fitted on the rest of Catalonia. In every test it ranked the held-out crashes better than the table, but its estimate of the fatal share missed for Barcelona, Girona and Tarragona, and fitted without Barcelona city it put the city's fatal share too low (5.9% estimated against 10.2% observed). The last year, 2023, was estimated at 11.5% against 11.9% observed. The model should not be used for a place it was not fitted on.

Limitations. The model describes crashes recorded in Catalonia in 2010–2023 on roads with a named owning network or of another type; the crashes on roads whose owner is recorded as “other” or left blank are left out. “Fatal” means a death within 24 hours, the Catalan definition; Spain's official figures count deaths within 30 days. The estimates are shares of recorded severe crashes, not the chance that a person in a crash dies.
