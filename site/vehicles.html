Vehicle risk per kilometre

A heavy truck is in a fatal crash 10.3 times as often as a car per vehicle on the road, and 2.5 times as often per kilometre driven. The gap between those two numbers is the difference between blaming the vehicle and describing how much it is used. Motorcycles move the other way. They are driven little, so a modest rate per vehicle becomes 10 times a car's rate once distance is the divisor.

Per kilometre, the motorcycle's excess is in how often it crashes: 8.56× a car's injury crashes, of which a share only 1.18× a car's is fatal. The heavy truck is the opposite: it is in 0.42× a car's injury crashes per kilometre, but 5.91× as many of them are fatal.

Who dies in the crash

The last column of the first table is the second half of the finding. When a motorcycle is in a fatal crash, 0.93 of its own riders are killed on average; for a car 0.52; for a heavy truck 0.18. A fatal crash kills at least one person, so a figure of 0.18 means that in most fatal crashes involving a heavy truck the people killed were in the other vehicle or on foot. A truck's risk per kilometre is mostly a risk to other people, which is exactly what a measure built from its own occupants' deaths would miss.

Conclusion

Per vehicle on the road a heavy truck is in a fatal crash 10.3× as often as a car; per kilometre, 2.5×, because each truck is driven 4.1× as far. Per kilometre the motorcycle leads, at 10×, almost all of it from crashing 8.56× as often; the truck's 2.5× is all deadliness, and its own occupants are 0.18 of the deaths in each fatal crash it is in, so most of the people it kills are in other vehicles or on foot: a measure built from a vehicle's own occupants would miss most of the truck's toll.

Limits. Kilometres exist for 2022 only, so this is a cross-section, not a trend. They are modelled from inspection odometer readings, and DGT's own note says they are valid for aggregates rather than for individual vehicles. They are annualised over readings taken across 2014 to 2023, so they describe a normal year imputed to the 2022 fleet rather than 2022 travel. The two sides of the division do not cover quite the same vehicles: the crash counts include foreign-registered vehicles, and the kilometres include the distance Spanish vehicles drive abroad. Vans and light trucks are one group because the crash record and the register split them differently; taken apart, light trucks would show 39% of a van's rate per kilometre, a gap with no plausible cause but the coding. The intervals come from the crash counts and treat the kilometres as known.
