Road deaths in July and August run 1.22× and 1.14× the average month. Divided by road fuel sold, the only monthly traffic series that covers every road and every vehicle, they are 1.12× and 1.09×: smaller, but both still above the average month, outside their intervals. Per tonne of road fuel, February to May are below the average month, and September above it. Road fuel is fuel sold, not kilometres driven, so these are month effects per tonne of fuel, not per kilometre.

No series counts kilometres on all Spanish roads month by month. Road fuel sold (petrol plus diesel) covers every road and every vehicle and is the one series used here as a denominator. Two narrower series are shown beside deaths as traffic indices only: petrol sold, which leaves out every diesel vehicle, and average daily traffic per kilometre on the state toll motorways, a small part of the network. They do not move together. In August the deaths index stands at 113, road fuel sold at 105 (down from 109 in July), petrol sold at 116 (up from 115) and toll-motorway traffic at 140. Which of them is closest to kilometres driven on all roads is not something these data can establish, so neither narrow series is used to divide all-road deaths.

With year effects taking out the trend, July's deaths are 1.22× the average month as a count and 1.12× per tonne of road fuel (interval 1.06 to 1.18); August's are 1.14× and 1.09× (1.03 to 1.15). Per tonne of road fuel, February to May run between 0.90 and 0.92, and September 1.10; the other months are not distinguishable from the average month.

The 2020 lockdown

In April 2020, the one full month of the strictest lockdown, deaths fell 68% on the 2017–2019 average. Road fuel sold fell 58%, petrol sold 76% and toll-motorway traffic 78%: deaths fell by more than road fuel and by less than the two narrower series. Per tonne of road fuel, deaths fell 24%. Without a monthly count of kilometres on all roads these series cannot say whether each kilometre driven that month became more or less dangerous, and this page does not claim either.

Conclusion

Per tonne of road fuel sold, the only monthly traffic series that covers every road and vehicle, July and August stay above the average month (1.12× and 1.09×, against 1.22× and 1.14× as a count): the summer excess is smaller per tonne of fuel than as a count, but it does not disappear. February to May are below the average month per tonne of fuel, and September above it. These are rates per tonne of fuel sold, not per kilometre driven. In April 2020 deaths fell 68%, by more than road fuel sold and less than petrol sold or toll-motorway traffic; the series cannot tell whether the roads became more or less dangerous per kilometre that month.

Limits. Road fuel is fuel sold by month, not fuel burnt, and it mixes freight with private travel, so a month effect per tonne is not a month effect per kilometre. Petrol sold and toll-motorway traffic are shown only as traffic indices: petrol leaves out diesel vehicles, and the toll network is a small part of the roads that shrank as concessions expired, which is why its intensity per kilometre is read rather than vehicle-kilometres. Monthly deaths are small counts, so single months carry wide intervals; the month effects pool 9 years from 2014 to 2024, with 2020 and 2021 left out.
