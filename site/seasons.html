Over time

Seasonal variation in road deaths

Road deaths in Spain peak in summer: July has 1.22 times the deaths of the average month and August 1.14 times. Per tonne of road fuel sold, the only monthly measure of traffic on all roads, July and August stand at 1.12 times and 1.09 times the average month, still above it, but other measures of traffic disagree about August. In April 2020, under the lockdown, deaths fell 68% and road fuel sold 58%.

Per tonne of fuel, July to September are above the average month and February to May below it

Each month is compared with the average month of the same year, so the long-run trend does not enter the pattern. Per tonne of road fuel, the 95% intervals for July (1.06–1.18) and August (1.03–1.15) lie above 1. February to May lie between 0.90 times and 0.92 times the average month, with intervals below 1; September, at 1.10 times, has an interval above 1. The intervals of the other months include the average month.

The measures of traffic disagree about August, so the excess per kilometre is unknown

No series counts the kilometres driven on all Spanish roads month by month, so road fuel sold stands in for them (road fuel as a measure of traffic). It peaks in July and falls in August, while petrol sold, which leaves out diesel vehicles, and traffic on the state toll motorways, a small part of the network, peak in August.

In the average August deaths stand at 113 on this index, road fuel sold at 105, petrol sold at 116 and toll-motorway traffic at 140. Which comes closest to the kilometres driven on all roads is unknown, and so is how much of the summer excess would remain per kilometre.

In the April 2020 lockdown, deaths fell more than road fuel sold and less than petrol or motorway traffic

April 2020 was the one full month under the strictest restrictions. Against the average April of 2017–2019, deaths fell 68%, road fuel sold 58%, petrol sold 76% and toll-motorway traffic 78%, so deaths per tonne of road fuel fell 24%. Petrol and toll traffic were already above their 2017–2019 level before the lockdown, so measured from early in the year their falls would be larger still. The comparison in detail is in the technical notes.
