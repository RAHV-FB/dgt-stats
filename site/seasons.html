Seasonality and mobility

Is the summer peak in road deaths a peak in danger, or in driving? Divide each month by three measures of traffic and see what is left.

The summer rise in road deaths is mostly more driving. Deaths in July and August run 1.22× and 1.14× the average month. Per unit of petrol, which in Spain is burnt mostly by private cars and motorcycles, they are 1.06× and 0.98×: August's excess disappears and July's shrinks to a few per cent. What survives the allowance for traffic is elsewhere in the year: spring has fewer deaths per unit of traffic under every measure, and September and November more under at least two of the three.

No series counts kilometres on all Spanish roads month by month, so three stand in for it, each wrong in a known direction. Road fuel includes freight diesel, which slows in the August industrial holiday, so it rises only to 105 in August. Traffic on the state toll motorways, mostly long-distance holiday routes, rises to 140. Petrol sits between them at 116. The truth for the whole network lies somewhere in that range, which is why the answer is given under all three.

With year effects taking out the trend, July's deaths are 1.22× the average month as a count, 1.12× per unit of road fuel and 1.06× per unit of petrol; August's are 1.14×, 1.09× and 0.98×, and per unit of toll-motorway traffic 0.80×. April and May run between 0.88 and 0.96 under every proxy. September and November carry more deaths per unit of traffic under at least two of the three, and the winter months under some. That is consistent with longer hours of darkness: in the microdata for 2024, darkness holds 25% of interurban injury crashes but 34% of interurban deaths. These series do not show darkness to be the cause.

The lockdown as a natural experiment

In April 2020, the one full month of the strictest lockdown, deaths fell 68% on the 2017–2019 average. Road fuel fell 58%, petrol 76% and toll-motorway traffic 78%. The fall in deaths was the size of the fall in traffic. Whether each remaining kilometre became more or less dangerous depends on which proxy is believed: per unit of road fuel deaths fell 24%, per unit of petrol they rose 35%. The data cannot settle it, and this page does not claim either.

Conclusion

Most of the summer peak in road deaths is a peak in driving: per unit of private-car fuel, August is an ordinary month and July only slightly worse. The seasonal pattern that is not explained by volume runs the other way from the headlines: spring is safer per unit of traffic and autumn riskier. The 2020 collapse in deaths was a collapse in traffic, and is not evidence that the roads became safer.

Limits. The traffic series are proxies: fuel sales by month, not fuel burnt, and toll traffic on a network that shrank as concessions expired, which is why intensity per kilometre is used rather than vehicle-kilometres. Monthly deaths are small counts, about 100 to 200, so single months carry wide intervals; the month effects pool nine years.
