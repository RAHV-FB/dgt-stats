Over time

Seasonal variation in road deaths

Road deaths in Spain peak in summer: compared with an average month of the same year, July has 1.22 times the deaths and August 1.14 times. More road fuel is sold in those months, and per tonne of road fuel, the one monthly stand-in for traffic that covers all roads, July and August stand lower, at 1.12 times and 1.09 times the average month, with 95% intervals wholly above it. On the same basis February to May are below the average month and September above it. In April 2020, under the lockdown, deaths fell 68% and road fuel sold 58% against the average April of 2017–2019.

Deaths peak in July, and the measures of traffic disagree about August

No series counts the kilometres driven on all Spanish roads month by month. Road fuel sold (petrol plus diesel) is the one series used to divide deaths, standing in for kilometres as on the annual pages (road fuel as a measure of traffic). Two narrower series are shown beside deaths only: petrol sold, which leaves out every diesel vehicle, and traffic on the state toll motorways, measured directly but on a small part of the network, as vehicles per kilometre of motorway. The three series diverge in summer. In the average August, deaths stand at 113 on this index, road fuel sold at 105 (down from 109 in July), petrol sold at 116 (up from 115) and toll-motorway traffic at 140. Which of them comes closest to the kilometres driven on all roads is unknown.

Per tonne of fuel, July to September are above the average month and February to May below it

Per tonne of road fuel, the 95% intervals for July (1.06–1.18) and August (1.03–1.15) lie above 1. February to May lie between 0.90 times and 0.92 times the average month, with intervals below 1; September, at 1.10 times, has an interval above 1. The intervals of the other months include the average month.

Each month is compared with the average month of the same year, so the long-run trend does not enter the seasonal pattern. The intervals allow for monthly counts that vary more than chance alone would produce. Both versions pool nine years from 2014 to 2024, leaving out 2020 and 2021.

In the April 2020 lockdown, deaths fell more than road fuel sold and less than petrol or motorway traffic

April 2020 was the one full month under the strictest restrictions. Against the average April of 2017–2019, deaths fell 68%, road fuel sold 58%, petrol sold 76% and toll-motorway traffic 78%, so deaths per tonne of road fuel fell 24%. Both narrower series were already above their 2017–2019 level before the lockdown: in January and February 2020 petrol sold was 13% and 16% higher and toll-motorway traffic 9% and 10% higher. The toll network had also shrunk, from 2,513 km on average in the April of 2017–2019 to 1,894 km, as concessions expired. Measured from their level early in the year, the April falls of petrol and toll traffic would be larger still, so deaths still fell less than both.

Limitations. None of the three series measures kilometres driven on all roads, and the summer excess reads differently against each. Against road fuel sold, deaths rise more than fuel sales in July and August; against toll-motorway traffic they rise less in both months; against petrol sales they rise more in July and less in August. How much of the summer excess would remain per kilometre is unknown.
