Model 924e1606d1d6, fitted on 22,638 crashes recorded in 2010–2023. How it was built and tested

The calculator needs JavaScript. The severity model page gives its estimates for typical crashes.

The estimate is the model's share of fatal crashes (a death within 24 hours) among crashes already recorded with a death or serious injury in Catalonia, not the chance that a crash happens. Its 95% interval covers only the uncertainty in the model's coefficients, not the differences between places and years. Tested on later years in Catalonia, the only region it was tested in, its estimates missed in some years and places, which the severity model page names.

The description of the crash sets the model's inputs: its type, the kinds of road user involved and how many vehicles and pedestrians there were. Conditions that cannot occur together are not offered: heavy rain, hail or snow on a dry road, daylight at 00:00–05:59 or 22:00–23:59, and dawn, dusk or night at 10:00–13:59.

The crashes the model was fitted on leave out the 1,840 on conventional roads whose owning network is not named. How the model was built and tested is on the severity model page and in the calculator report.

Road safety in Spain, an independent analysis by Russell Howard (RAHV-FB). Data from the Dirección General de Tráfico, INE, the Ministerio de Transportes, CORES, the Servei Català de Trànsit, the Ajuntament de Barcelona, the Autoritat del Transport Metropolità, Idescat and Institut Metròpoli (EMEF), Idescat (population census) and the Consorcio Regional de Transportes de Madrid (Powered by CRTM). All results are computed by the code in the repository from the published files and the cited values it holds.
